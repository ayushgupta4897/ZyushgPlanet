# 00 · Executive summary

**The milestone.** Do what Kuber Mehta did (found 2026-10-10, announced 2026-10-11) (find an unreported transiting-planet signal in archival space-telescope data with an AI coding agent), but in a way that survives a referee.

**What his find really is.** A real, repeating, on-target transit signal (P = 13.769 d, ~2 ppt, 70 events) on a faint red dwarf that was never a Kepler target, pulled out of the 2023 Kepler Bonus background-star photometry. It is a *candidate* with a 6.9 % false-positive probability, it cannot be validated with any telescope an amateur can reach, his RNAAS note was declined in a day, and his own inverted-light-curve controls showed his first screen passed noise as often as data. The tweet said "planet"; the repo says "candidate". (`docs/01`, `01b`, `03`)

**The wave he is part of.** Between 2026-09-25 and 2026-10-16 at least twelve AI-agent or citizen archival searches went public; one (eumemic) swept ~4.17 million TESS dwarfs. The community-TOI portal has accepted only published candidates since August. The verified open-ground ranking put the non-M Kepler Bonus background dwarfs first for novelty and faint nearby TESS M dwarfs first for validatability. (`docs/02`, `04b`)

**What we built.** A frozen, hashed target list of 6,465 Kepler Bonus background dwarfs that were never Kepler targets and have no published transit search; a pre-registered two-stage screen with an inverted-flux null on every star; positive controls that caught three real bugs before any pool star was judged; a pixel-level localisation test checked on Kuber's star (one positive control); a Modal deployment driven over HTTPS (the sandbox cannot speak gRPC); ephemeris matching against every known object; and a repo-level rule set (`CLAUDE.md`). (`pipeline/`, `docs/05`, `08`, `09`)

**What we found (2026-10-17).** 6,465 stars: 6,365 on Modal in 2 h 17 m (`runs/poolA_main/modal_status.json`) and 100 locally; 31 had no usable cadences. First screen: 448 real vs 97 inverted passes, but the excess sits almost entirely at 20–40 d (379 vs 31), the signature of a dip-shaped gap systematic that an inverted null cannot mimic, so the null bounds only sign-symmetric false alarms. Stage 2: 11 survivors under the registered rules (v1.4), 23 under a post-hoc revision (v1.5) that drops an ill-defined narrow-window TLS statistic and adds an empirical-SNR floor; both are reported. Pixel localisation (chi-square model comparison, validated on Kuber's star) puts the missing light on the target for 7 of the 23, on another star for 8, and is inconclusive for 8. Independent re-derivation plus red-team review (batch A, four signals) found no false-positive mechanism for any of the four. Two are candidate-grade in the workflow's sense: kic-006699887 (15.11 d, ~1,020 ppm, ~2.3 R⊕ if real, K dwarf at 859 pc; a second 47.69-d signal on the same star) and kic-003831969 (15.31 d, ~1,000 ppm, ~2.8–4 R⊕). Two need work: kic-009520080 (2.377 d, the strongest and best-localised signal, but with an unexplained ~200 ppm brightening at phase 0.5 that fails the two-sided secondary rule) and kic-006448637 (35.59 d, localised only by red-team scratch code because its pixel stamp is dominated by a saturated star). Of the other re-derivations finished so far, five agree with the pipeline and four disagree, every disagreement pointing at post-gap ramps or single-cadence outliers in the 21–40 d band; one of them exposed a stage-2 hole (a pass with one usable event) that is now closed by a post-hoc minimum-event rule (v1.7, 23 → 22 survivors). Nothing is called a planet. (`docs/10`)

**How close.** Two signals have cleared re-derivation, red team, pipeline pixel localisation and a documented TRICERATOPS run, so under this repo's rules they are *candidates* (three so far): kic-006699887 with FPP 2.4 % ± 2.1 % and kic-003831969 with FPP 2.7 % ± 0.3 % (NFPP 0 for both; 8 seeds × 10⁶ draws, `runs/poolA_main/fpp/`; deep single-target runs agree with stage 2; evidence packs with SHA-256 manifests under `candidates/`). Neither is below the 1.5 % validation line, and in both the residual is a planet orbiting an unresolved bound companion, which only high-resolution imaging can shrink. A third, kic-011138423 (4.89 d, ~2.1 R⊕ on an early-M dwarf at 894 pc), is also a candidate with FPP 3.6 % ± 0.2 %; kic-003963519 and kic-009655224 have cleared the red team and await their FPP. The adversarial pass is complete for all 23 stage-2 survivors: of the 15 that were red-teamed, 7 survive, 1 is weak (host ambiguous inside 1″) and 7 were rejected, and the 8 with an off-target pixel verdict were not sent. Every rejection is a post-gap ramp, a single-cadence spacecraft event, a saturated-neighbour or off-stamp PSF artefact, a same-channel eclipsing binary or a background eclipsing binary; two were Kepler targets that leaked into the pool through the Gaia–KIC cross-match (`docs/10` §1 lessons). To a *validated planet*: far, for the same reason as Kuber (hosts at ~1 kpc, G 16–17). To a *validatable* find: that is Pool B (faint nearby TESS M dwarfs), already scoped. (`docs/07`, `10`)

**Honest caveats.** The pixel test uses an empirical point-source model, not the official Kepler PRF, and many background targets sit at the edge of the host star's pixel stamp (those quarters are now marked inconclusive); per-event errors understate correlated noise (calibrated on controls); the stage-2 criteria were registered after a 208-star partial look at the batch and revised three times afterwards (all logged in the prereg revision log); host radii are colour-based estimates; a Goddard/KBonus team expects "several hundred" candidates from this same dataset and may publish first.
