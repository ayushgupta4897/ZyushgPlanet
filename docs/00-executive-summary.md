# 00 · Executive summary

**The milestone.** Do what Kuber Mehta did (found 2026-10-10, announced 2026-10-11) (find an unreported transiting-planet signal in archival space-telescope data with an AI coding agent), but in a way that survives a referee.

**What his find really is.** A real, repeating, on-target transit signal (P = 13.769 d, ~2 ppt, 70 events) on a faint red dwarf that was never a Kepler target, pulled out of the 2023 Kepler Bonus background-star photometry. It is a *candidate* with a 6.9 % false-positive probability, it cannot be validated with any telescope an amateur can reach, his RNAAS note was declined in a day, and his own inverted-light-curve controls showed his first screen passed noise as often as data. The tweet said "planet"; the repo says "candidate". (`docs/01`, `01b`, `03`)

**The wave he is part of.** Between 2026-09-25 and 2026-10-16 at least twelve AI-agent or citizen archival searches went public; one (eumemic) swept ~4.17 million TESS dwarfs. The community-TOI portal has accepted only published candidates since August. The verified open-ground ranking put the non-M Kepler Bonus background dwarfs first for novelty and faint nearby TESS M dwarfs first for validatability. (`docs/02`, `04b`)

**What we built.** A frozen, hashed target list of 6,465 Kepler Bonus background dwarfs that were never Kepler targets and have no published transit search; a pre-registered two-stage screen with an inverted-flux null on every star; positive controls that caught three real bugs before any pool star was judged; pixel-level localisation tests (a per-quarter χ² model comparison and a season-stacked version, v1.8) checked against positive and negative controls (KIC 5689121 and kic-006699887 on target; kic-006367225 and kic-001577049 offset); a Modal deployment driven over HTTPS (the sandbox cannot speak gRPC); ephemeris matching against every known object; an independent re-derivation and red-team review for every surviving signal; evidence packs with SHA-256 manifests; and a repo-level rule set (`CLAUDE.md`). The pre-registration (`pipeline/prereg_poolA.json`, now v1.8) logs every change in its `revision_log`. (`pipeline/`, `docs/05`, `08`, `09`)

**What we found.** 6,465 stars: 6,365 on Modal in 2 h 17 m (`runs/poolA_main/modal_status.json`) and 100 locally; 31 had no usable cadences. First screen: **448 real vs 97 inverted (2 − f) passes**, but the excess sits almost entirely at 20–40 d (379 vs 31), the signature of a dip-shaped gap systematic that an inverted null cannot mimic, so the null bounds only sign-symmetric false alarms. Stage 2: 11 passes under the registered rules (v1.4), 23 under a post-hoc revision (v1.5), and **22** after a post-hoc minimum-event rule (v1.7) closed a hole that let a one-event signal through; all three counts are reported. Pixel localisation puts the missing light on the target for **5** of the 22 (kic-003831969, kic-003963519, kic-006699887, kic-010213563, kic-011138423). Independent re-derivation plus red-team review leaves **4** (kic-010213563 is a real event whose light comes from an unmodelled Gaia neighbour inside 1″). Across all 16 signals sent to the red team, 7 survived, 1 was weak and 8 were killed, the kills pointing at post-gap ramps, single-cadence outliers, a saturated neighbour, a channel-wide eclipsing binary and a background eclipsing binary. Nothing is called a planet. (`docs/10`, `report/data.json`)

**How close.** Three signals have cleared independent re-derivation, red team, pipeline pixel localisation, a deep single-target run, a transit fit whose implied stellar density matches Gaia (within 0.5 σ), human inspection of the plots (`runs/poolA_main/human_review.json`) and a documented TRICERATOPS run, so under this repo's rules they are **candidates**: KIC 6699887 (15.1136 d, 2.76 R⊕), KIC 3831969 (15.3134 d, 3.47 R⊕) and KIC 11138423 (4.8945 d, 2.83 R⊕). Their false-positive probabilities are 2.4 %, 2.7 % and 3.6 % with default priors and **0.63 %, 0.71 % and 0.70 %** with TRICERATOPS 1.1.0, Gaia FLAME host parameters and MOLUSC companion constraints from Gaia RUWE and a Gaia contrast curve; NFPP is 0 throughout. KIC 6699887 also carries a second on-target signal at 47.69 d (own FPP 2.4 %); it is counted as a second signal on that star, not a fourth candidate. All three are below the 1.5 % statistical-validation threshold on Gaia constraints alone, but none is *validated*: that needs a refereed paper, and a referee will most likely ask for one high-resolution image per host. The fourth survivor, KIC 3963519, stays a *signal*: its FPP is ≈ 39 % because of an unmodelled G ≈ 18 neighbour. An RNAAS note describing the three candidates was submitted on 2026-10-18 (AAS82486, under consideration); data, code and evidence are archived at Zenodo, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23442225. (`docs/10`, `candidates/`)

**Honest caveats.** The pixel tests use an empirical point-source model, not the official Kepler PRF, and many background targets sit at the edge of the host star's pixel stamp (those quarters are marked inconclusive); per-event errors understate correlated noise (calibrated on controls); the stage-2 criteria were registered after a 208-star partial look at the batch and revised three times afterwards, and the pixel rules twice (v1.4–v1.8, all logged in the prereg revision log and labelled post-hoc); TRICERATOPS uses a 3×3-pixel aperture as a stand-in for the Kepler Bonus PSF photometry, and its seed-to-seed scatter is large for KIC 6699887 (0.15–3.4 %); the transit-fit chains are short (about seven autocorrelation times); a Goddard/KBonus team expects "several hundred" candidates from this same dataset and may publish first.
