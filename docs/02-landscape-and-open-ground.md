## 0. Headline corrections to the earlier brief
1. Kepler Bonus background light curves are NOT an untouched planet-search dataset. (a) Bienias & Szabó 2025, A&A 694, A209 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09152) report 7 hot-Jupiter candidates (0.89–1.52 RJup, P 2.5–4.8 d) among Kepler background stars. (b) A 2026 NExScI workshop poster by Julie Moquin, R. F. Wilson, E. Quintana, C. Hedges, J. Martínez-Palomera, Z. Claytor, E. Gilbert, J. Rowe, K. Hoffman, V. Kostov, D. Ciardi ("One Man's TraSHDUMP is Another Man's Treasure: An Exoplanet Candidate in the Kepler Bonus Light Curves", https://vmnexsci.ipac.caltech.edu/workshop/2026/posters/Poster_JulieMoquin_41.pdf) presents BOI-1.01 (P = 3.37 d, 2.94 ± 0.15 Re, host G = 17.8 M dwarf, 0.51 Rsun), found with a Kepler-style matched-filter TCE pipeline (TraSHDUMP), Robovetter-like flux vetting (RECYCLEBin) and pixel localisation (INCInERATOr), and states an "expected yield of several hundred new planet candidates" from KBonus. The KBonus authors' own team is therefore running a systematic planet search; scoop risk over months is real (none of this is yet a paper).
2. KBonus counts: 27,378 is the number of ALL background sources passing Mehta's cuts (PSFFRAC > 0.5, CROWDSAP > 0.7, ≥ 8 PSF quarters, kic ≠ tpforg), including the 1,836 M-catalogue stars. Non-M-catalogue background sources passing the cuts = 25,542 (bp_rp < 0.75: 733; 0.75–1.2: 17,087; 1.2–2.0: 7,449; > 2.0: 273). G < 14: 1,257; G 14–16: 4,894 [recomputed from hlsp_kbonus-bkg source + mstars catalogues, https://archive.stsci.edu/hlsp/kbonus-bkg]. Mehta's chain 29,800 → 9,204 → 6,477 → 2,495 → 1,836 reproduces exactly; relaxed cuts (PSFFRAC > 0.3, CROWDSAP > 0.5, ≥ 6 quarters) give 6,105 M stars (5,284 background); 2,040 M-catalogue stars are Kepler primary targets (659 pass the cuts).
3. Ten more agent-era projects exist (section 1b). Most consequential: Avijit Thawani's 1,098-star search at 60–100 pc (Chopra's pipeline, FFI-only), whose two preregistered candidates "Niyati" and "Avijit" were retracted on 2026-10-13 as neighbour eclipsing binaries 33″ and 47″ away (https://github.com/avijit-thawani/tess-niyati-avijit); and coracle-us/duo-transits (2026-10-11), which is recovering periods of Hawthorn et al. 2024 duotransits with new sectors "aiming for a Research Note of the AAS" (https://github.com/coracle-us/duo-transits).
4. TESS DDT "Program 100" for TIC 4206066 appears in Rabtsevich's preregistration (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23175179: "TESS observes the star again in Sector 110, from 2026-10-31 to 2026-11-26, at 2-minute cadence") and in news (neoteo.com), but the public DDT list (https://tess.mit.edu/public/DDTs/DDT_list.html) ends at DDT 099 on 2026-10-17. Treat as "per Rabtsevich", not officially listed.
5. Mehta's Kepler search was run with Codex, not Claude Code: his post reads "Found a new planet with Codex this weekend" (https://x.com/kuberwastaken/status/2109240782368751834, 2026-10-11 11:12 UTC; 9,650 views / 239 likes / 8 RT / 15 replies / 4 quotes via api.fxtwitter.com). https://kuber.studio/planethunt/ credits Codex for the search and "ChatGPT Work" for follow-up; Claude Code is not credited on the site. The site also states 1,736 searches = 1,648 unique KICs (the brief said 1,736 stars).
6. "CTOI-only hosts have never been searched for extra planets": overstated. eumemic's full-sky sample is "every FGKM dwarf in TIC 8.2 with T ≤ 13.5, Teff ≤ 6,500 K and R ≤ 1.5 R☉ that our earlier searches had not covered" with "Up to 3 signals per star ... by iterative masking" (README lines 487, 737). Bright CTOI-only hosts were therefore searched, unmasked. The residual gap is CTOIs whose known signal is a single/long-period event that BLS cannot remove, and the 1,397 FP/FA-only TOI hosts (mostly EBs).
7. Comdex4's agent use is broader than "PR #8": seven merged PRs are titled "Claude/tess transit hunter repo alu4hv" (#1, 2, 5, 6, 8, 14, 21; 2026-09-24 to 10-06; all 23 PRs by Comdex4) [https://github.com/Comdex4/tess-transit-hunter/pulls?q=is%3Apr].
8. The ExoFOP policy date 2026-08-19 comes only from Chopra's docs/CTOI_DRAFT.md; the ExoFOP page is undated and the Wayback Machine has no 2026 snapshot. ## 5. Open ground, re-ranked for a solo 4-CPU developer with Claude Code (4-week horizon)
Scoring: (i) expected real, unreported candidates per 1,000 stars searched; (ii) chance of validation (FPP < 0.015, NFPP < 0.001) or at least independent recovery; (iii) scoop risk within 4 weeks; (iv) compute/IO on 4 CPUs.

1. Kepler Bonus background non-M dwarfs with G < 17 (~10,500 sources passing Mehta's cuts; 25,542 in total) plus the relaxed-cut background M dwarfs (5,284) and 659 PSF-rereduced primary M targets, 0.5–100 d. (i) High: the KBonus team itself expects "several hundred" candidates across the catalogue; Kepler's own yield was ~27 KOIs per 1,000 targets, so ~3–10 per 1,000 is plausible for G < 17 at ≥ 2 Re. (ii) Low for validation (hosts ~1 kpc, G 14–17), but KBonus held-out quarters, PSF-vs-SAP depth, PRF centroids and Kepler-169-style ephemeris nulls give strong internal tests; novelty is checkable against KOI/TCE/EB tables, Bienias & Szabó's 7 and BOI-1. (iii) Low over 4 weeks (Goddard team is at poster stage; Mehta has stopped), moderate over months. (iv) Trivial: ~17 s/star BLS, a few GB of light curves. This is also the most direct replication of Mehta's milestone.
2. TESS M dwarfs with T ≈ 13.5–16 within 100 pc (103,730 Gaia M dwarfs at G 14–16.5), especially the northern half that T16 (Cycle 1 south) never covered. (i) Moderate: M-dwarf occurrence is high, but with 2–5 sectors only ≥ 2 Re at P < 10 d is detectable (order 0.5–1 per 1,000). (ii) Moderate: multi-ppt depths are reachable from 1-m telescopes; TRICERATOPS works. (iii) Moderate: Kunimoto/LEO-Vetter and T16 extensions are natural owners; nobody in the agent cohort has gone below T = 13.5. (iv) The deciding factor: per-star TESScut extraction for 10^5 stars is ~1 TB of cutouts, too heavy; first check whether the TGLC HLSP (Han & Brandt 2023, G < 16) covers the needed sectors; if it does, a 20k-star pilot (< 60 pc) is a weekend job.
3. TESS 30–200 d, duo and single transits on FGKM dwarfs with ≥ 10 sectors (continuous viewing zones), T ≤ 13.5. (i) Low–moderate per 1,000 (geometric transit probability falls as P^-2/3) but each hit is scientifically valuable. (ii) Hard to validate; each candidate gives a falsifiable ephemeris that Year 9 sectors (S108–121) can test. (iii) Moderate–high: eumemic issue #1 proposes a joint single-transit search; coracle-us is already recovering Hawthorn 2024 duotransit periods; eumemic's 20–100 d search covered 6,932 stars. (iv) Moderate: ~10^4–10^5 stars × 10–40 sectors of light curves (hundreds of GB); restrict to K/M dwarfs T ≤ 12.5 with ≥ 12 sectors.
4. Masked re-search of CTOI-only hosts whose known signal is single/long-period, plus the 1,397 FP/FA-only TOI hosts, and all 4,116 CTOI-only hosts with eumemic's --mask-tois stack-slide. (i) Low–moderate: eumemic already searched these stars unmasked with up to 3 iteratively masked signals, so the marginal gain is small except where a deep or single-event signal spoiled detrending. (ii) Good: bright hosts (median T 11.6), many with ExoFOP imaging. (iii) High: eumemic can add this in one build. (iv) Trivial (~9 s/star).
5. Newest sectors S105–S108 (and S109 as FFIs arrive) with custom FFI photometry of nearby dwarfs, Rabtsevich-style "find it new, confirm it archival". (i) Moderate for FFI-only stars never in QLP/TESS-SPOC multi-sector searches (TESS-SPOC ends S96, QLP S104, SPOC TCE search S96). (ii) Moderate: archival recovery over 3–7 yr is the test. (iii) High once QLP/TESS-SPOC deliver S105+. (iv) Heavy: ~30k nearby dwarfs per sector × ~9 MB TESScut ≈ 270 GB per sector; do one sector.
6. K2 Campaigns 0–19: 1,372 open candidates, no agent-era search found, professional completeness weak at Kp > 15 and P > 40 d; TESS revisits the ecliptic for joint fits.
