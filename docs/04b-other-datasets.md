## 0. Headline findings (read this first)

1. **"Searched" has three different meanings and the gaps are mostly in the second and third.** (a) *Detection* searches (TPS/BLS run on a light curve), (b) *vetting* into a public candidate list (TOI/KOI/CTOI), and (c) *novelty* catalogues you must cross-match. For TESS the detection step is near-complete to T = 13.5 (QLP, TESS-SPOC, SPOC) and the vetting step is badly bottlenecked; for faint stars (T > 13.5) even the detection step is patchy. For Kepler the primary-target search is saturated, but the Kepler Bonus background sources are a largely unsearched ~425k-source pool that one Goddard/Ames team has just started mining.
2. **TESS right now (2026-10-17):** "TESS is in Orbit 229, Sector 109. Sector 110 begins on October 31, 2026" (https://tess.mit.edu/observations/). Cycle 9 / Year 9 began with Sector 108 on 2026-09-07 (HEASARC). SPOC 2-min light curves and single-sector DV/TCE products are public through **Sector 107** (13,000 observations released 2026-10-01, 24 days after the sector ended; MAST query, sandbox). **No TOIs have been alerted from Sectors 103-107** (0 of 8,151 TOIs list any of those sectors; ExoFOP). Newest single-sector SPOC TOIs are from S102 (alerted 2026-08-06); newest QLP faint-search TOIs from S98 (2026-07-24). Measured lags: data public 24-39 d after sector end; SPOC single-sector TOIs 80-148 d; QLP faint-search TOIs 200-338 d. QLP S99-104 carry a known barycentric-timing error and are still flagged "not yet redelivered" (QLP HLSP page updated 2026-08-12). **TESScut already serves FFI cutouts for S108 and the in-progress S109** (sandbox), so the freshest data are reachable only by building your own photometry.
3. **The under-searched TESS regime, quantified:** the TIC holds **100,567 dwarfs with Teff < 3900 K within 100 pc and 13.5 < T < 16.5**; only **33** are TOI hosts and **43** are CTOI hosts (sandbox). Only **457 of 8,151 TOIs** and **351 of 5,138 CTOIs** have T > 13.5; only 35 TOIs and 44 CTOIs have T > 15. eumemic's full-sky sweep stopped at T <= 13.5; Chopra's covered 526 stars. **But this pool is not product-free:** TESS-SPOC's "within 100 pc" rule puts ~1,900-3,800 of these stars into each sector's 160k-target list (including 500-1,100 with T > 15), QLP lists hold ~3,000-3,300 per sector (none fainter than T = 15), and a random 60-star census found 51 with at least one public product and 9 with none (sandbox, Section 4.5). The gap is in *vetting* (33 TOI hosts) and in *uniform multi-sector coverage*, not in raw photometry.
4. **Kepler Bonus beyond Kuber's slice:** his funnel reproduces exactly from the public v1.0 catalogues (29,800 -> 9,204 -> 6,477 -> 2,495 -> 1,836; sandbox). The *same* quality cuts on the non-M background sources leave **25,542 stars** (6,151 with G < 16; 10,417 with G < 17) with no published transit search. Caveat: a NASA Goddard/Ames team (Moquin, Wilson, Quintana, Hedges, Martinez-Palomera, Claytor, Gilbert, Rowe, Hoffman, Kostov, Ciardi) presented "BOI-1.01" and the TraSHDUMP matched-filter pipeline at the 2026 NExScI workshop, expecting "several hundred new planet candidates" from Kepler Bonus.
5. **K2 is more searched than it looks, but has structural holes:** 361,770 unique EPIC targets across C0-C19; Kruse 2019 (C0-8, Kp 9-15, 818 candidates) and Zink 2021 (C1-8, C10-18, 747 candidates, 61% maximum completeness) are the systematic sweeps. Holes: C0 and C19 (outside Zink's uniform sample), Kp > 15, periods > ~20-40 d, and no Kepler-Bonus-style Gaia-driven PSF-deblended product for K2 (only cluster-superstamp PSF work: Libralato 2016, Nardiello 2016 with 3 M67 candidates, Soares-Furtado 2017).
6. **Nothing else is competitive for a solo developer in 2026:** Gaia DR4 goes live 2 Dec 2026 12:00 CET with epoch photometry for ~2.8 billion sources but only ~68 field-of-view transits per star over 66 months (DR3 sample scaled; sandbox) and DPAC publishes its own `vari_planetary_transit` table (1,007 rows planned); ground surveys (ZTF 8-25 mmag repeatability, ASAS-SN to V ~ 17, ATLAS 2-day cadence) only reach hot-Jupiter depths; CHEOPS is targeted (1-yr proprietary); **PLATO launch is planned for January 2027** (ESA; not March); Roman launched 30 Aug 2026 and is commissioning.
7. **Ranked recommendation (Section 9):** (1) faint (13.5 < T < 16) M dwarfs within 100 pc, all sectors, own FFI photometry filling the per-sector gaps in TESS-SPOC/QLP/TARS, with the fresh-sector window as a bonus; (2) the fresh-sector window itself via the public TCE-statistics CSVs (636 S107 TCEs on non-TOI stars) and TESScut S108-109; (3) the 25,542 non-M Kepler Bonus background stars (novel, cheap, but un-validatable at G > 16 and contested); (4) K2 C0/C19/faint/long-period residue; (5) everything else only as cross-check source.

## 1. Vocabulary: three kinds of "searched"

| Layer | What it means | Where the gaps are |
|---|---|---|
| **Detection** | A transit-search algorithm (TPS, BLS, TLS, QATS, ML) was run on a light curve of this star. | TESS: complete to T <= 13.5 via QLP (S1-104) and SPOC/TESS-SPOC; patchy for T > 13.5 (TGLC S1-55, eleanor-lite S1-26, T16 Cycle 1 with the Roth 2026 search, TARS S1-96 light curves but no transit search). Kepler: complete for 198,709 primary targets; Kepler Bonus background: only the M-dwarf slice (Kuber, 1,736 stars) and an in-progress Goddard search. K2: Kruse/Zink cover Kp <~ 15, C1-18. |
| **Vetting/publication** | The signal was examined and put on a public list (TOI, CTOI, KOI, published candidate table). | TESS: QLP TCEs below T = 10.5 were "not considered for vetting" in the prime mission (Guerrero 2021); the faint-star search (Kunimoto 2022) extended this to 13.5; SPOC single-sector TCEs become TOIs 80-148 d after sector end; QLP faint-search TOIs 200-338 d. |
| **Novelty cross-match** | Catalogues you must check before claiming "unreported". | Listed in Section 7 with counts. |

The practical consequence: a signal can be present as a SPOC TCE in the public `tcestats` CSV (detected) and still be "unreported" in the TOI sense. eumemic exploited exactly this: they "re-measured the 14,112 TCEs on 8,698 FGKM dwarfs that are not TOIs or CTOIs" (eumemic README). Kuber's star was never a target of any pipeline, so it was unreported at *all three* layers (sandbox: TIC 120686465 has TARS light curves in 10 sectors and no other TESS product of any author; its only Kepler product is the KBONUS-BKG file).

## 2. Kepler prime mission (2009-2013)

### 2.1 What exists
- **Targets and search:** The DR25 pipeline run searched **198,709 stellar targets** and produced **34,032** transit signals/TCEs (Twicken et al. 2016, arXiv:1604.06140, abstract; TAP `q1_q17_dr25_tce` = 34,032 rows). Thompson et al. 2018 (ApJS 235, 38; arXiv:1710.06758) catalogued **8,054 KOIs, 4,034 planet candidates** with periods 0.25-632 d; completeness > 85% and reliability > 98% for P < 100 d. The cumulative KOI table today holds **9,564 rows: 2,748 CONFIRMED, 1,977 CANDIDATE, 4,839 FALSE POSITIVE** (TAP: `select koi_disposition, count(*) from cumulative group by koi_disposition`).
- **Cadence:** 29.4-min long cadence for all targets; ~512 short-cadence (58.8 s) slots.
- **Full-frame images:** calibration FFIs "collected approximately monthly" (Montet et al. 2017, arXiv:1705.07928, abstract). Useless for transit detection.
- **Superstamps:** Kepler read out 200x200-pixel long-cadence superstamps of NGC 6791 and NGC 6819 for the whole mission (Kuehn et al. 2013, arXiv:1309.6044; the abstract describes variable-star work only). Later uses: red giants via difference imaging (Espinoza-Rojas et al. 2023, arXiv:2310.06757). **No published transit search of these two superstamps was found** (literature search 2026-10-17; negative result, medium confidence). Members are at ~2.5-4 kpc, G >~ 17-19: un-validatable.

Both low-yield. Do not spend compute here.

### 2.3 Kepler Bonus (KBONUS-BKG) - the actually open Kepler pool
Facts (MAST HLSP page https://archive.stsci.edu/hlsp/kbonus-bkg, "Updated: 2023-11-01", fetched 2026-10-17; Martinez-Palomera, Hedges & Dotson 2023, AJ 166, 265, arXiv:2310.17733; DOI 10.17909/7jbr-w430; CC BY 4.0):
- "606,900 light curves, from which 406,548 correspond to background sources and 200,352 are Kepler targets"; Gaia DR3 input catalogue; PSF photometry on Kepler TPFs; "CDPP values under 100 ppm for sources brighter than G = 16". In the v1.0 source catalogue, `kic != tpforg` gives **425,219** rows and `kic == tpforg` 181,681 (sandbox; the page's 406,548/200,352 split uses a different definition).
- **Catalogue columns (source catalogue, 606,900 rows, 24 columns):** `gaia_designation, ra, dec, sap_mean_flux(_err), psf_mean_flux(_err), flfrcsap, crowdsap, npixsap, psffrac, pertrati, pertstd, psf_avail, sap_avail, psf_fraction_flag, phot_g_mean_mag, phot_bp_mean_mag, phot_rp_mean_mag, tpforg, kic, kic_sep, kep_mag, fname`. `psf_avail` and `sap_avail` are 18-character '0/1' strings (one per quarter Q0-Q17), **not** integers: count quarters with `.count('1')`. The **M-stars catalogue has only 5 columns** (`gaia_designation, ra, dec, kic, fname`; 29,800 rows, all of which match the source catalogue on `gaia_designation`) - every quality cut must be done after a join (sandbox).
- **Light-curve file (verified by download of Kuber's star):** named by KIC even for a background source: `hlsp_kbonus-bkg_kepler_kepler_kic-005689121_kepler_v1.0_lc.fits`. HDUs: `PRIMARY`, `LIGHTCURVE_STITCHED`, then `LIGHTCURVE_Qn` / `APERTURE_Qn` pairs for each available quarter (13 for this star: Q1-4, 6-8, 10-12, 14-16). Columns: `TIME, CADENCENO, QUARTER, FLUX, FLUX_ERR, SAP_FLUX, SAP_FLUX_ERR, PSF_FLAT_FLUX, PSF_FLAT_FLUX_ERR, SAP_QUALITY, FLATTEN_MASK`; 50,182 cadences; header `OBJECT='KIC 5689121'`, `TPFORG=5689116`, pipeline `VERSION='1.1.2'`, `HLSPVER='V1.0'`. lightkurve: `lk.search_lightcurve(SkyCoord(285.907827,40.941988,unit='deg'), author='KBONUS-BKG', radius=20*u.arcsec)` returns the target (`Gaia DR3 2103792461826460288`, distance 0.0") and its neighbour (`Gaia DR3 2103792427466721792`, 3.1").
- **Catalogues:** source (page says 140 MB; the file is 159,022,080 bytes), M-stars (page says "~20,000 sources" and 2.0 MB; the file has **29,800 rows**, 2,390,400 bytes), KOIs+neighbours ("~32,000" light-curve files, 2.6 MB), white dwarfs (~90). URLs: `https://archive.stsci.edu/hlsps/kbonus-bkg/catalogs/hlsp_kbonus-bkg_kepler_kepler_{source,mstars,kois-nns,wd}-catalog_kepler_v1.0_cat.fits`. Bulk light curves 4.8 TB total; M-star bundle `hlsp_kbonus-bkg_kepler_kepler_mstars_kepler_v1.0_lcf.tar.gz` is 134 GB (HLSP page).
- **Reproduced funnel (sandbox, join on `gaia_designation`):** M-star catalogue 29,800 -> PSFFRAC > 0.5: 9,204 -> CROWDSAP > 0.7: 6,477 -> >= 8 PSF quarters: 2,495 -> `kic != tpforg`: **1,836**. Identical to Kuber's audit. G-distribution of the 1,836: 2 at 13-14, 10 at 14-15, 24 at 15-16, 68 at 16-17, 341 at 17-18, 1,391 at 18-19 (36 with G < 16). Kuber's star in the catalogue: `kic 5689121, tpforg 5689116, psffrac 0.666, crowdsap 0.747, flfrcsap 0.688, npixsap 2, 13 PSF quarters, G 16.28, kep_mag 16.33, kic_sep 0.067"` - i.e. it *is* KIC 5689121, a catalogued KIC star that was simply never a Kepler target. 27,495 of the 29,800 M-star rows have a KIC number.
- **Unsearched complement (sandbox):** same cuts on the 577,100 non-M sources -> 275,659 -> 192,698 -> 132,009 -> **25,542 background sources**; G bins <13: 320, 13-14: 937, 14-15: 1,824, 15-16: 3,070, 16-17: 4,266, 17-18: 6,712, 18-19: 8,412. Median BP-RP 1.06 (K-ish). **6,151 have G < 16** (CDPP < 100 ppm regime). 107,126 primary-target rows pass the same cuts (searched by DR25). Files: `../other-datasets/kbonus/kbonus_nonM_background_survivors.csv`, `kbonus_M_background_survivors.csv`.
- **Kuber's extra step** ("exclude primary targets", 1,836 -> 1,800) removes sources that were Kepler targets in some quarter. Practical implementation (my suggestion, not from his code): drop any source whose `kic` appears anywhere in the set of `tpforg` values of the source catalogue, or check MAST Kepler holdings per KIC; note TAP `q1_q17_dr25_stellar` is "not available for querying" (sandbox) while `keplerstellar` (990,244 per-quarter rows) is.
- **Caveats:** median `kic_sep` of background survivors is 0.23", so most "background" sources are KIC entries never selected as targets. Blending with the originating target is the dominant false-positive mode (Kuber's candidate sits 7.1" from a 2.1x brighter star); per-quarter aperture-depth vs. flux-fraction tests are mandatory.
- **Competition:** Moquin et al., "One Man's TraSHDUMP is Another Man's Treasure: An Exoplanet Candidate in the Kepler Bonus Light Curves" (2026 NExScI workshop poster, https://vmnexsci.ipac.caltech.edu/workshop/2026/posters/Poster_JulieMoquin_41.pdf, HTTP 200): "triple the number of planet-search stars", "an increase in the number of M dwarfs by a factor of seven", "an expected yield of several hundred new planet candidates"; BOI-1 is "a 17.8 G mag M-dwarf" with a 3.37-d, ~2-h TCE at 7.1 sigma MES, Rp 2.94 +/- 0.15 Re, vetted with RECYCLEBin (Robovetter-like), INCInERATOr (pixel PRF localisation) and TRICERATOPS-PLUS; "High resolution AO imaging rules out the Background Transiting Planet scenario". Expect a BOI catalogue from this group.
- **Sister product:** KBONUS-APEXBA (https://archive.stsci.edu/hlsp/kbonus-apexba; DOI 10.17909/t9-d5wy-e535; Martinez-Palomera et al. 2022, AJ 163, 93; updated 2021-12-13): 9,327 sources in the Kepler EXBA background super-apertures. (The brief's "69 EBs and one 0.81 RJ candidate" is from the paper, not the page [not re-verified].)

## 3. K2 (2014-2018)

### 3.1 What exists
- **Targets:** NASA Exoplanet Archive `k2targets` holds **392,245 target-campaign rows, 361,770 unique EPIC IDs** (TAP). Per campaign (TAP, from the original brief, not re-run): C0 7,748; C1 21,646; C2 13,401; C3 16,375; C4 15,781; C5 25,137; C6 27,289; C7 13,261; C8 23,565; C9 623 (microlensing superstamp campaign); C10 27,170; C11 13,607; C12 28,088; C13 21,367; C14 29,897; C15 23,278; C16 29,413; C17 32,221; C18 20,427; E 1,951. C19 is not a separate value in this table; C19 was cut short by fuel exhaustion (K2 handbook; the exact usable duration was not re-verified - the keplerscience.arc.nasa.gov host did not resolve from the sandbox).
- **Systematics:** 6-hour thruster-firing roll corrections dominate; Zink et al. 2021 attribute their 61% maximum completeness to "the heavy detrending K2 data require" (abstract).
- **Light-curve products on MAST** (K2 HLSP index https://archive.stsci.edu/k2/hlsps.html and product pages, fetched 2026-10-17):
  - **EVEREST** v2.0 (Luger et al. 2018; DOI 10.17909/T9501J): c00-c08, c102, c111, c112, c12-c18 (v2.0 C14-18 added 2019-04-19; v1.0 removed 2017-11-15); "precision comparable to the original Kepler mission for targets brighter than Kp = 13", within a factor of 2 fainter. lightkurve author `EVEREST` (sandbox: `lk.search_lightcurve('EPIC 201367065', author='EVEREST')` returns C1). **The K2 HLSP index page is stale** (lists EVEREST only to C13); trust the EVEREST page.
  - **K2SFF** (Vanderburg & Johnson): MAST "Campaigns 0 though 14 plus the pre-campaign 0 engineering targets"; Vanderburg's page https://lweb.cfa.harvard.edu/~avanderb/k2.html lists C0-C19 (9a/9b, 11a/11b). lightkurve author `K2SFF` (sandbox: returns C1 for K2-3).
  - **K2SC** C3-8 and C102 (lightkurve author `K2SC` returned 0 rows for K2-3/C1 - consistent). **POLAR** C1-6. **K2VARCAT** C0-4. **K2GAP** C1. **KEGS** C5,6,8. **K2SNE** C16-17. **HALO** 161 bright stars C4+. **PSFK2** (Libralato et al. 2016, **arXiv:1510.09180**): PSF photometry with neighbour subtraction in the C0 M35/NGC 2158 superstamp to Kp ~ 24 (10% in 6.5 h), 30 ppm at the bright end; page exists at https://archive.stsci.edu/hlsp/psfk2 (HTTP 200) though absent from the index.
  - **K2SUPERSTAMP** (Cody et al. 2018, arXiv:1802.06354): WCS-calibrated mosaics for M35 (C0), M67 (C5), Ruprecht 147 (C7), NGC 6530/Lagoon (C9). Published transit work on these fields: Nardiello et al. 2016 (arXiv:1608.08539, M67) report "three planetary candidates"; Soares-Furtado et al. 2017 (arXiv:1703.00030) image-subtraction on the C0 superstamp (35 ppm at Kp 12-12.25). So the superstamps are *not* unsearched, only incompletely searched.
  - **No K2 equivalent of KBONUS-BKG exists** (literature search 2026-10-17, negative result): no Gaia-catalogue-driven PSF deblending of every K2 postage stamp. K2 stamps are small, so the background-source yield per stamp is much lower than Kepler's, but non-zero and untouched.

### 3.3 What is left in K2
- **Campaign 0 and Campaign 19** (outside Zink's uniform sample; C0 covered by Vanderburg 2016/Crossfield 2016/Kruse 2019; C19 by nobody systematically, but it is short).
- **Faint stars, Kp > 15:** Kruse's sample stopped at Kp = 15; Zink searched EVEREST targets but completeness at Kp > 15 is low. K2 M dwarfs at Kp 15-17 within 100-200 pc are a plausible residue.
- **Long periods:** 80-day campaigns mean 2-transit detections for P > 40 d and single transits for P > 80 d; Osborn 2016 covered only C1-3 for singles.
- **Background/non-EPIC sources:** nothing published.
- **Verdict:** real but small residue; K2 fields are at ecliptic latitudes revisited by TESS, so a new K2 candidate on a Kp < 13 star would likely already be a TESS TCE/TOI. Check `toi`/`ctoi` before investing.

## 4. TESS (2018-present)

### 4.1 Mission status on 2026-10-17 (live checks)
- **Observing:** "TESS is in Orbit 229, Sector 109. Sector 110 begins on October 31, 2026" (https://tess.mit.edu/observations/). HEASARC sector table (https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/tess/sector.html): S97 2025-09-15 to 11-09 ("Cycle 8 begins; extended sector length"), S98 11-09 to 2026-01-05 (extended), **S99 listed twice (2026-01-05 to 01-15 and 01-22 to 02-02)** around the 3I/ATLAS special sector 1751 (01-15 to 01-22), S100 02-02 to 03-01, S101 to 03-27, S102 to 04-21, S103 to 05-17, S104 to 06-13, S105 to 07-11, S106 to 08-09, S107 to 09-07, **S108 2026-09-07 to 10-05 ("Cycle 9 begins", southern)**, S109 10-05 to 10-31, S110 10-31 to 11-26, S111 11-26 to 12-22, S112 12-22 to 2027-01-17, S113-114 to 2027-03-14 (south), S115 ecliptic from 2027-03-14. The MIT page calls this "Year 9" (Sectors 108-120/121) and never says "Cycle 9".
- **Extended mission:** "TESS's third extended mission (EM3) is expected to occur September 2025-September 2028" (Guide to TESS for EM planning, HEASARC PDF, local copy `../other-datasets/em_guide.txt` line 32). FFI cadence: 30 min (Prime, S1-26), 10 min (EM1, S27-55), 200 s (EM2 onward, S56+); "TESS is currently observing ~13,000 2-minute TPFs, ~2,500 20-second TPFs, and is collecting FFIs every 200 seconds" (same guide). MAST confirms exactly 13,000 SPOC 2-min time-series observations per sector for S105-107 (sandbox).

### 4.4 The TOI process and the measurable "TOI lag"
- Prime-mission process (Guerrero et al. 2021, arXiv:2103.12538; quotes verified in the local pdftotext): "The SPOC pipeline produces, on average, about 1,200 TCEs per sector, out of about 20,000 two-minute cadence targets observed. Before triage, QLP produces 50,000 TCEs per sector (with a Tmag = 10.5 cutoff for vetting)... Each sector yields about 100 total TOIs"; "Astronet-Triage ingests tens of thousands of TCEs brighter than Tmag = 13.5 and selects several thousand (3000-4000) targets... further reduced by setting a brightness threshold of Tmag = 10.5"; "TCEs from the QLP below the Tmag = 10.5 threshold were not considered for vetting". TOI `Source` strings today: `spoc-sNNN-b02` (single-sector SPOC), `spoc-s14-s86-b0A/b02/b0H` (multi-sector SPOC; 188 TOIs all-time with T up to 18.3, but the 25 alerted since 2026-07-01 span T 8.6-15.0, median 11.6 - this is **not** a faint-star stream), `qlp-sNN-ffi` and `qlp-sNN-faintsearch`.
- **TOI table today (TAP):** 8,151 TOIs; PC 4,820, CP 815, KP 606, FP 1,315, FA 100, APC 490, blank 5; latest `toi_created` 2026-10-08; 330 TOIs created in 2026 (Jan 69, Mar 23, Apr 18, Jun 133, Jul 49, Aug 35, Oct 3), 87 since 2026-07-01; median T of 2026 TOIs 12.50.

- **Interpretation:** at any moment ~4-5 public sectors have SPOC TCEs not yet turned into TOIs (currently S103-107: e.g. 636 S107 TCEs on non-TOI/CTOI stars), ~9 sectors lack QLP faint-star TOIs (S99-107; S99-104 additionally blocked by the timing bug), ~22 sectors (S86-107) lack TESS-SPOC FFI light curves, and 2 sectors (S108-109) exist only as FFIs/TESScut. - **Public light-curve coverage of this pool (sandbox):** random 60 stars -> 51 have >= 1 product (TESS-SPOC 38, TARS 50, QLP 32, eleanor-lite 26, TGLC 22, T16 20, TASOC 11, SPOC 2-min 7); 9 have no product at all. - **Bright stars the agent wave skipped:** eumemic's dwarf cuts (Teff <= 6,500 K, R <= 1.5 Rsun) exclude hot/early-F stars and subgiants. Stars with T <= 13.5 first observed in S105-109 are inside the TOI lag, but eumemic has searched S1-104/105 for dwarfs.
- **Period/duration regime:** TESS-SPOC FFI searches use a 2-h minimum pulse duration (vs 0.5 h for 2-min), so short-duration transits (1-1.5 h) of M dwarfs in FFI data are under-searched by SPOC - a documented gap favouring your own BLS/TLS with 0.5-1 h durations.

## 9. Ranked recommendation for a solo developer with 4 CPUs and Claude Code

**Rank 1 - Faint nearby M dwarfs in TESS (13.5 < T < 16, d < 100 pc, all sectors), with FFI photometry you control.** Why: 100,567 stars, 33 TOI hosts; small planets give 0.1-1% transits (median R = 0.23 Rsun: a 1.5 Re planet is ~3,500 ppm); eumemic's and Chopra's sweeps stop at T = 13.5 and 60 pc; SPOC's 2-h minimum FFI duration under-searches short M-dwarf transits; hosts are Ks ~ 10-12 so Keck/Palomar/SOAR speckle or AO, ground photometry and reconnaissance spectroscopy are feasible, which is what TRICERATOPS/MOLUSC needs to get below FPP 0.015 (eumemic got 255 of 645 to threshold "apart from high-resolution imaging"). How: start with the 6,196 stars within 50 pc (TIC criteria query, ~12 s), re-derive distances and radii from Gaia DR3 via the ExoFOP cross-match; for each star inventory every public product (TESS-SPOC, QLP, TGLC via a separate 3.12 env, TARS, eleanor-lite, T16) and the per-sector TESS-SPOC/QLP target lists, then build your own 200-s aperture + PRF-weighted photometry from TESScut for the sectors no product covers (through S109), so coverage is uniform; detrend (wotan biweight), TLS/BLS 0.5-30 d with durations down to 0.5 h, inverted-flux and shuffled-sector nulls, LEO-Vetter-style tests, per-sector centroid/difference-image localisation (eumemic rejected 93% of LEO-Vetter passes at the pixel stage - budget for that), then TRICERATOPS with Gaia-informed MOLUSC. Expected throughput: ~5-15 s per star-sector for cutout photometry + search on one core [estimate, not measured], i.e. the 50-pc sample in ~1-2 days of 4-CPU wall time at <= 5 TESScut requests/s; the full 100-pc sample is a cloud job. Risk: pixel confusion in 21" pixels; mitigate with Gaia DR3 neighbour lists and the Kostov EB list.

**Rank 2 - The fresh-sector TOI-lag window as a standing job.** Each new sector becomes public 24-39 days after it ends and TOIs follow 3-5 (SPOC) to 7-11 (QLP) months later; TESScut FFIs arrive weekly and already include S108-109. Today S103-107 are public with zero TOIs. Pull the `tcestats` CSV (e.g. S107: 846 TCEs, 636 on non-TOI/CTOI stars, 439 of them with SNR >= 9) the day it appears, cross-match against TOI/CTOI, re-fit and vet; run your own search on QLP (where unaffected by the timing bug) and on TESScut photometry for T > 13.5 stars. The bright end overlaps eumemic (through S104/105) and the TOI team, so prioritise the faint and the non-dwarf (Teff > 6,500 K, R > 1.5 Rsun) targets, and multi-sector combinations where a new sector pushes an old sub-threshold signal over SNR 9 (eumemic: "only 24 needed Sectors 97-104" while 47% already reached SNR 9 with S <= 55). Pre-register predictions on Zenodo as Chopra did.

**Rank 3 - Kepler Bonus non-M background stars (25,542; 6,151 with G < 16).** Cheapest novel pool: public PSF light curves, 4 years of 30-min cadence, CDPP < 100 ppm for G < 16, no published search. Run Kuber's exact pipeline (reproduced funnel above, after joining the catalogues on `gaia_designation`) with his inverted-flux null and his dilution/centroid tests; use `FLUX` (PSF) and `SAP_FLUX` per quarter from the `LIGHTCURVE_Qn` HDUs. Trade-offs: (i) a funded Goddard/Ames team with the data's authors is doing exactly this - move fast and publish as a Zenodo/RNAAS catalogue rather than one-candidate papers; (ii) at G > 16 a candidate is un-validatable (no AO, TESS too shallow, Kuber's FPP stuck at 0.069), so restrict to G < 15-16; (iii) the editor's objections to Kuber's RNAAS (sample construction, inverted-null parity, TESS non-detection, prior-search status) will apply to you - build the null comparison and the membership/target-list cross-check into the pipeline from day one.

**Rank 4 - K2 residue:** C0 and C19, EPIC stars with Kp > 15, and P > 40 d two-transit searches on EVEREST/K2SFF light curves; cheap because the light curves are small and `k2pandc` (4,077) is complete. Low yield; mostly a pipeline shakedown.

**Rank 5 - Everything else as vetting inputs, not hunting grounds:** ZTF/ATLAS/ASAS-SN forced photometry for EB/variability checks; Gaia DR3 (RUWE, NSS, variability classes, the corrected `vari_planetary_transit`) and DR4 epoch photometry after 2 Dec 2026 for ephemeris extension and blend checks; CHEOPS public visits only for known hosts.

**What not to do:** Kepler primary targets, Kepler/K2 superstamps, Gaia-only transit searches, ground-survey-only transit searches - saturated or physically incapable of yielding a validatable small planet.

## 10. Open questions and caveats
- Whether TARS light curves preserve transit depth and shape after `unpopular` CPM detrending is untested; an injection-recovery on a few hundred TARS stars is a prerequisite.
- The faint-M pool's per-sector coverage was measured for three TESS-SPOC sectors (S31, S69, S85) and two QLP sectors (S85, S104) plus a 60-star random census; a full 100,567 x 109-sector coverage matrix should be built from all target-list CSVs before choosing which stars need home-made photometry.
- QLP S99-104 redelivery date is unknown (page updated 2026-08-12 still shows asterisks; the Aug-2026 MAST newsletter says products "will be replaced"). Whether lightkurve/MAST indexes QLP S98-104 for a given star could not be confirmed (TIC 150428135 showed QLP only to S97).
- Whether the Goddard TraSHDUMP/BOI search has already covered the non-M Kepler Bonus background (the poster shows only BOI-1); email the poster authors before investing weeks.
- The HLSP page's "~20,000" M-star statement vs the file's 29,800 rows: the file is authoritative; note it in any paper.
- Planet Hunters TESS current activity (Zooniverse page shows no dated news) and whether Kostov's ~900k candidate list exists outside the ApJS supplements.
- Precision figures for QLP at T = 13.5 and for ASAS-SN/ATLAS at V ~ 12-14 are from secondary sources or memory; treat as approximate until read from the papers' noise figures.
- PLATO launch month: ESA says January 2027 (via search results; ESA pages blocked from the sandbox); a news outlet says March 2027.
- Roman GBTDS start date is not published (overview slide places early seasons in 2027-2028).
- K2 Campaign 19 usable duration was not re-verified (keplerscience.arc.nasa.gov did not resolve).
