## 0. Headline findings

1. **The CTOI shortcut is closed.** ExoFOP paused all community-candidate uploads on **31 March 2026** ("CTOIs and other projects") and reopened on **19 August 2026** for "community planet candidates from TESS, Kepler, K2, and beyond" with a new rule: *"candidates must first be published in a peer-reviewed journal with online access before being uploaded to ExoFOP, and approval to upload candidates is required"* (https://exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/tess/news.php). The Candidate Guidelines add: *"Candidates can also be presented in a Research Note of the American Astronomical Society (RNAAS) if the methodology to detect and vet the candidate has been previously published in a peer-reviewed journal that is available online and is cited by the Research Note"* (https://exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/tess/candidate_help.php). The CTOI table downloaded 2026-10-17 (5,138 rows) has **0** rows with a paper URL modified before 2026-08-19, then **1,173** rows on 2026-08-27 (user `lafargamagro`, Lafarga et al. 2026 MNRAS, RAVEN) and **1** row on 2026-10-08 (user `mason`, TIC 392664683.01, Paper = https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/2515-5172/aeaee1, note "PHT Community Candidate"). That last row is the template to copy.
2. **RNAAS is in scope for single candidates and does accept sole-author, independent, AI-assisted notes.** The 2017-10-16 scope statement lists *"the discovery of a single exoplanet"* (https://journals.aas.org/news/scope-of-research-notes-of-the-american-astronomical-society/). Precedents in 2026: Kaplan (published 2026-01-15, sole author, Sivas Science and Technology University, TESS M-dwarf candidate TIC 22760643.01, DOI 10.3847/2515-5172/ae3758, no AI disclosure) and Rennell (published 2026-07-31, *"Independent Researcher, Anchorage, Alaska, USA"*, acknowledgment *"OpenAI Codex assisted with code generation, reproducibility checks, and manuscript drafting; the author reviewed the evidence and accepts responsibility for the analysis and text"*, DOI 10.3847/2515-5172/ae91dc). So Kuber's decline was not because the venue rejects candidates, independents or AI disclosure per se. (Correction to earlier drafts: Kaplan is university-affiliated; only Rennell is both independent and AI-disclosed.)
3. **Why AAS82222 was declined is not recorded in Kuber's repo.** `publication/2026-10-13/submission-record.json` holds only `outcome: "Declined"`, date 2026-10-13, *"Decision letter supplied by the author in this conversation; not independently checked in the portal"*, `scientific_editor: "Not Assigned"`. Timing inference (new): the portal shows submission at "12-Oct-2026 16:20:10" and the record's `verified_at_utc` is 2026-10-12T20:21:13Z, so the portal clock is US Eastern and submission was ~20:20 UTC; the post-decision audit was committed 2026-10-13 12:55 IST (07:25 UTC). The decline therefore arrived within **~11 hours**, before a Scientific Editor was assigned — a moderator ("appropriateness and format") decision, not a review. His audit addresses (a) sample construction, (b) the other 30 first-screen signals, (c) the TESS non-detection, (d) the Barber et al. 2022 Table 5 match — by inference the editor's concerns.
4. **NASA Exoplanet Archive ingests only accepted, peer-reviewed papers.** *"The results must be peer reviewed and be accepted for publication in the astrophysical literature"* (https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/exoplanet_criteria.html); *"All data or values must come from a peer-reviewed and accepted publication"* (https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/contribute_data.html, last update 10 December 2025 — note the older `docs/contribute.html` URL is a 404). KOI tables are frozen at Q1–17 DR25 (KOI docs page last updated 25 September 2018). TAP check 2026-10-17: KIC 5689121 / TIC 120686465 / Gaia DR3 2103792461826460288 appears in none of `ps`, `toi`, `cumulative`.
5. **The realistic route for a Kepler-Bonus background-star candidate**: (i) Zenodo *Preprint* + *Dataset* records with DOIs the day the ephemeris survives your null tests; (ii) a refereed *methods + candidate* paper (AJ/MNRAS/PASP) or a refereed methods paper followed by an RNAAS that cites it, ideally co-authored with a professional; (iii) only then ExoFOP "Community Candidate" upload with Discovery Data Source = Kepler (no precedent yet: all 5,138 current rows are TESS-source); (iv) NEA ingestion follows acceptance automatically; a Kepler number comes only for a *confirmed or validated* planet via exonames@ipac.caltech.edu.

### 1.3 Likely editorial reasons (inference, ranked; none documented)
- **Substance/scope at the moderation gate**: RNAAS is *"moderated by an editor for appropriateness and format before publication"* and *"not peer reviewed"* (https://journals.aas.org/research-note-preparation-guidelines/); the lead editor is Chris Lintott (chris.lintott@aas.org; https://journals.aas.org/research-notes/). A ≤11 h decline with no editor assigned is a moderation decision. The note arrived with FPP = 0.069 (not validated), a null control that passes noise as often as data, a TESS non-recovery, an unresolved prior-search question, and an unrefereed pipeline.
- **The note undercut itself**: a large fraction of the 1,203 words are caveats (*"not an independent holdout test"*, *"not calibrated probabilities"*, *"Novelty remains provisional"*, *"The evidence warrants further investigation, not a confirmed-planet claim"*). Honest, but a moderator may conclude there is no communicable result yet.
- **Public-claim/manuscript mismatch**: tweet *"Found a new planet ... went undiscovered"* vs manuscript *"I do not assign the association's age or claim that the source was never previously searched."* Speculative as a cause, but a known reputational pattern (§7).
- **AI/authorship**: the manuscript discloses *"OpenAI ChatGPT/Codex and Anthropic Claude assisted the research workflow, including code, analysis and scientific synthesis"* and *"The human author is responsible ... AI tools are not authors."* This complies with the AAS LLM guidelines (posted 9 September 2026). Rennell's note shows Codex-assisted independent notes are accepted, so disclosure alone is unlikely to be the cause; but "scientific synthesis" by AI plus a sole author with no track record raises the bar on whether a human stands behind the analysis.
- **Format**: not a factor (1,203 < 1,500 words incl. references; one multi-panel figure; abstract 124 < 150; first-person singular; AASTeX 7).

### 1.5 Live RNAAS and AAS policy (checked 2026-10-17)
- **Scope** (16 Oct 2017): *"works in progress, comments and clarifications, null results"*, timely observation reports, results that *"would not traditionally merit a full paper (such as the discovery of a single exoplanet ...)"*.
- **Limits** (https://journals.aas.org/research-note-preparation-guidelines/): *"1,500 words or fewer, with no more than a single figure or table (but not both)"*; *"The 1,500 word count limit includes title, headers, captions, and references"*; *"150 words reserved for the required abstract"*; *"the AAS Editorial office's word count is the final arbiter"*; *"typically published within 72 hours of manuscript receipt"*; *"free to read and currently carry no author publication charges"*; CC-BY 4.0; *"A limit of twenty Notes in any 12 month period by a given first author"*; *"style for single author papers is first person singular"*; *"please do not use line numbers"*; *"preferably with AASTeX v7+"*; *"in most cases, RNAAS is unable to publish substantially novel theories."* Main page: *"non-peer reviewed, indexed and secure record of works in progress"*, *"moderated but not edited"*, *"searchable in ADS and fully citable"*.
- **Volume**: Crossref lists **288** RNAAS items published 2026-01-01 → 2026-10-17 (`api.crossref.org/journals/2515-5172/works?filter=from-pub-date:2026-01-01,until-pub-date:2026-10-17&rows=0`). No RNAAS by Rabtsevich, Chopra or Kuber Mehta as of that date.
- **AI policy** (https://journals.aas.org/author-llm-guidelines/, *"initially posted on 9 September 2026"*): *"At submission, authors are required to disclose whether or not AI/LLMs were used in manuscript preparation"*; *"LLMs may not be used to generate any portion of a manuscript unsupervised"*; *"AI cannot be listed as an author"*; *"Publishing fictitious references is regarded as a serious professional breach"*; disclosure statement *"at the end of the article"*; figure captions must *"include the specific tool used, and, if possible, the prompt"*; *"these guidelines concern the manuscript, not the data analysis"*; consequences *"up to and including retraction"* and *"banning"*. Templates include the GAIDeT form (*"the following tasks were delegated to GAI tools under full human supervision: ..."*; generator at panbibliotekar.github.io/gaidet-declaration/).
- **Ethics** (https://journals.aas.org/professional-and-ethical-standards-for-the-aas-journals/): authorship for those who *"contributed significantly to the inception, design, execution, or interpretation of the research"*; *"The list of authors establishes accountability as well as credit."* No affiliation requirement; Kuber's "Independent researcher" was accepted by https://aas.msubmit.net/.
- **arXiv and RNAAS** (https://journals.aas.org/news/rnaas-on-arxiv/, letter dated 17 April 2019): *"the arXiv is simply enforcing an existing policy of requiring submissions to be at least three pages long"*; arXiv: *"not accept short submissions of three pages or less, but there are always exemptions."* Authors *"added an extra paragraph and a figure (or more)"* to get posted. An RNAAS note may not be arXiv-postable as-is.

## 3. The ExoFOP Community-candidate (CTOI) route in 2026

### 3.1 Registration (https://exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/account.php)
*"You need to be conducting astronomical observational, modelling, or theoretical work at a level consistent with publication in a professional journal and relevant to follow-up studies of exoplanet candidates. Please include the name of the university, research center or similar facility with which you are affiliated."* Form: First/Last name, **Affiliation (required)**, Position {Faculty/staff, Postdoc, Grad student, Undergrad, **Citizen Scientist**, Other}, Email, Phone. *"registration is not instantaneous. You will be contacted by email with a user name and temporary password once your request is approved."* *"By requesting an account, you agree to the ExoFOP Professional Conduct Policy."* All uploads are public. TFOP WG membership is separate (§6).

### 3.2 Submitting (https://exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/tess/candidate_help.php)
- Step 0: *"complete the Published Candidate Upload Request. After approval, then you can submit candidates."* (https://exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/tess/pub_candidate_upload_request.php → "You must be logged in to view this page.")
- *"ExoFOP will only allow upload of Community Candidates accepted and published in the refereed literature. The URL link to the paper must be provided."*
- Two types: **cTOI** (TESS discovery source) and **Other Discovery Data Source** (*"discovered by projects other than TESS"*).
- Required: TIC-based name `TID.nn` (next in sequence); Discovery source (TESS default; uncheck and enter e.g. "Kepler"); optional Candidate Name (*"should not be assigned a lowercase letter ('b', 'c', etc) until they are confirmed in the published, refereed literature"*); disposition PC or FP (CP not allowed); **at least two of** period (d), transit midpoint (BJD), depth (ppm) >0; **for TOI-WG consideration all four of period, epoch, depth, duration**; a Tag; **Paper URL (required)**; descriptive Note (required); then upload the light-curve figure under the same tag. Cross-check TOIs, CTOIs and confirmed planets; duplicates removed, repeat offenders suspended; integer-multiple periods → add parameters to the existing object. Initial parameters are frozen; updates go in as additional parameter sets. Contact exofop-support@ipac.caltech.edu.

### 3.3 What happens after
- Guerrero et al. 2021 (arXiv:2103.12538, §8.4, PDF text): *"The TOI team periodically checks for matches on TIC ID and period between the CTOI list hosted on ExoFOP and the comprehensive collection of TCEs"*; *"169 CTOIs from the Prime Mission are now TOIs; 106 of these were recovered from TCEs mistakenly ruled out in pre-vetting triage"*; **"All CTOIs which have been promoted to TOIs were also TCEs in the SPOC pipeline or the QLP"**; CTOIs with no SPOC/QLP data products *"would need to be recovered manually in a reprocessing step"*; ~40% of 841 prime-mission CTOIs matched TEV TCEs. §9.1: *"The CP category includes planets with confirmed masses and statistically validated planets that do not yet have measured masses."* The TOI FAQ (https://tess.mit.edu/toi-releases/toi-release-faqs/) still says *"If you find a planet candidate submit it to ExoFOP-TESS. Your candidate will become a community TOI (cTOI). The candidate will be reviewed by the TESS TOI Team where if it meets the team standard it will be assigned TOI number"* — stale relative to the Aug-2026 rule.
- Live numbers (CTOI CSV 2026-10-17): 5,138 CTOIs, **814 promoted to TOI (15.8%)**; TFOPWG dispositions blank for 5,131. Promotion by submitter: `eisner` 119/182 (65%); `olmschenk` 165/184; `montalto` 155/1,076; `mason` 2/9; `kaplan` 0/4. AI-wave TICs 4206066, 370612437, 252721481, 120686465 are in neither the CTOI nor the TOI list (8,151 TOI rows, max TOI 7927.01).
- **Credit rule (TFOP WG Publication Policy v21, 12 July 2026, https://tess.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/TFOPWG_Publication_Policy_v21_20260712.pdf)**: *"In the special case of a community TOI (cTOI) that has been identified first by a member of the community and has been submitted to ExoFOP more than two months prior to its posting by the TESS project, the person or representatives of the team who provided or contributed to providing the cTOI should be invited to join as an author on any publication pertaining to that cTOI for the first time. The cTOI must have included a transit epoch and transit duration (and optionally an orbital period) that coincides with the transit in the publication. The two-month period captures the timeline of the typical internal process for vetting and releasing TOIs by the TOI WG."* This is the only written credit guarantee — and filing now requires a prior publication.

### 3.4 Worked examples

## 4. Kepler/K2 candidates in 2026: is there a channel?

- **No live KOI process.** KOI docs (last updated 25 September 2018) list Q1–17 DR25 as the latest delivery; the cumulative table is *"a superset of KOIs from all available quarters"* (https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/Kepler_KOI_docs.html). Nothing can be added.
- **The channel that works: refereed paper → NEA → Kepler number.** NEA TAP (2026-10-17), `ps`, `disc_facility='Kepler'`, `default_flag=1`, `disc_year>=2022`: Valizadegan et al. 2022 (301) and 2023 (69) = **370 ExoMiner planets**, Bouma 2022 (2), Cañas 2022/2023, Leleu 2022 (2), Sun 2022 (2), Goldberg 2023, Wu 2023, Weiss 2024, Wang et al. 2024 MNRAS 534, 1913 (5), Nabbie et al. 2025 Nat. Astron. (2), Ofir et al. 2025 AJ 169, 90 (2), Sun et al. 2025 Nat. Astron. (1), Lafarga et al. 2026 MNRAS (1). All became Kepler-/KOI-/TOI-named entries. So an ML/agent-validated set of Kepler candidates in a refereed journal **does** get ingested and named.
- **Kepler naming** (https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/KeplerNumbers.html, last updated 24 January 2022): use *"KOI number, KIC/KepID, or Kepler-XX b"* placeholders; *after acceptance* email exonames@ipac.caltech.edu with the acceptance letter, accepted manuscript, object list and planets per star; *"Kepler numbers will be assigned within two business days"*; eligibility *"confirmed or validated"*, borderline cases decided by NExScI science staff. The page assumes KOI/KIC designations; a KBONUS background source with a KIC entry (KIC 5689121 has one) probably qualifies, Gaia-only background sources are unresolved — email exonames.
- **K2** (https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/K2Numbers.html): candidates are `EPIC NNNNNNNNN.NN` numbered by the discoverer (check `k2pandc`; never reuse); K2 numbers only for confirmed/validated planets in accepted peer-reviewed papers. `k2pandc` TAP 2026-10-17: `disp_refname` 'Zink et al. 2019' (an RNAAS) = 16 rows, 'Zink et al. 2021' = 152; **but disposition CANDIDATE has max disc_year 2022** — no candidate catalogue (RNAAS or otherwise) has been ingested since; only CONFIRMED rows (Kanodia/Livingston/Sulis 2024, Damasso 2025, Collier 2026) were added. Treat the RNAAS→candidate-table path as historical.
- **NEA definitions** (https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/faq.html): *"There is not a separate disposition for validated planets"*; *"All planets added to the Exoplanet Archive are categorized as confirmed according to the archive's exoplanet criteria"*; `soltype` ∈ {Published Confirmed (20,729 rows), Published Candidate (2,197), Kepler Project Candidate (…), TESS Project Candidate (1,555)}; letters *"assigned alphabetically, starting from b, typically in the order the planets are published"*.
- **Kepler Bonus background hosts**: TAP shows no Kepler-facility planet on a Gaia-DR3-named host. Non-Kepler/KOI-named Kepler hosts are PH1, PH2, KIC 10001893 (Silvotti 2014), 2MASS J19383260+4603591 (Baran 2015), KIC 10525077/5437945/9663113/3558849 (Wang 2015; Morton 2016), KIC 7917485 (Murphy 2016), KIC 5479689/10068024/8121913 (Lillo-Box 2021), TOI-4588 (Lafarga 2026). A KBONUS-background validated planet would be a first. The KBONUS paper (arXiv:2310.17733; AJ 166, 265; 606,900 light curves, 406,548 new background sources) reports no candidates; authors Jorge Martínez-Palomera (BAERI/NASA Ames), Christina Hedges (NASA GSFC/UMBC), Jessie Dotson (NASA Ames) — 2023 affiliations; Martínez-Palomera is active in 2026 (RNAAS 10.3847/2515-5172/ae43bd on TESS 3I/ATLAS products, with Amy Tuson).

## 7. Naming and claiming

- **Candidate**: any transit-like ephemeris with basic vetting. ExoFOP: *"Planet candidates should not be assigned a lowercase letter ('b', 'c', etc) until they are confirmed in the published, refereed literature."* Use `TIC NNNN.01`, `KIC NNNN.01`/`EPIC NNNN.01`, or "a planet candidate associated with Gaia DR3 …".
- **Validated**: FPP < 0.015 and NFPP < 0.001 (TRICERATOPS, Giacalone et al. 2021, AJ 161, 24; valid only for Rp < 8 R⊕) or other FPP < 1%, **with** the constraining observations actually in hand, **and** published in a refereed journal. NEA then lists it as confirmed (*"There is not a separate disposition for validated planets"*) and assigns the letter; Kepler/K2/TESS numbers are assigned by NExScI/the TESS project after acceptance, never by the discoverer.
- **Confirmed**: dynamical mass (RV/TTV) or equivalent; the TESS "CP" class *"includes ... statistically validated planets that do not yet have measured masses"* (Guerrero 2021 §9.1).
- **Named**: IAU: *"The scientific designation of the exoplanet is the catalogue name of the host star followed by a letter"*; *"no unified naming convention for exoplanets exists"*; public names only via NameExoWorlds (https://iauarchive.eso.org/public/themes/naming_exoplanets/). You do not get to name it.
- **"New"/"undiscovered"**: claim absence only from *named* catalogues on a *stated* date (Kuber's manuscript did; his tweet did not). Rabtsevich's "nobody knew existed" was contradicted in print within a week (flagged 2020; LEO-Vetter early 2026).
- **Reputational mechanics observed this month**: the tweet-vs-repo gap (*"Found a new planet"* vs *"No confirmed planet is claimed"*) is exactly what moderators check; LLM-styled prose draws hostile attention regardless of content; inconsistent statements about AI use draw more; a public RNAAS decline becomes part of the record (Kuber's README line 1). Conversely, *"No statistical validation is claimed"* + DDT + quoted professionals produced goodwill for Rabtsevich even though the candidate was already in the TESS pipeline.
- **Wording ladder**: "transit-like signal" → "planet candidate (unvalidated)" → "statistically validated planet candidate (FPP = x, inputs y)" → "validated planet" (after refereed acceptance) → "confirmed planet" (after mass). Never skip a rung in a title, tweet, Zenodo title or README.

## 8. Concrete submission plan for a Claude-Code-found archival candidate

**Phase A — day 0 (ephemeris survives your own null tests)**
1. Zenodo: a *Preprint*-type record (PDF note) and a separate *Dataset* record, CC-BY, cross-linked; include P, T0 (BJD_TDB) with uncertainties, predicted future transit windows (UTC, barycentric-corrected), depth/duration, host IDs, vetting table, environment lock. Reserve the DOI; version later.
2. Pre-register the tests you will run on any *future* data (TESS sector, DDT) before they exist (Chopra's `*_predicted_transits.csv` is a template).
3. Do **not** tweet "planet". If you post, post the DOI with "candidate" and the FPP.

**Phase B — weeks 1–4: make it admissible**
4. Email one collaborator group (§6) with the evidence pack; request arXiv endorsement only once someone has read the work.
5. If the host is TESS-observable **and** the signal is TESS-detectable, submit a **TESS DDT** (2-min cadence; ≥6 weeks before sector start, late submission ≤2 weeks possible "under certain circumstances"; template asks only "Proposer #1 (Institution | email)"; tess-ddt@mit.edu; no proprietary period; approved list is public). Free, public, and it worked for Rabtsevich. For a G=16 Kepler-field star it will not help (~987 ppm increment vs 1,294 ppm scatter).
6. Run TRICERATOPS with *measured* inputs; if FPP < 0.015 & NFPP < 0.001 and Rp < 8 R⊕ you have validation-grade evidence; otherwise write it as a candidate and name the observation that would close it (AO contrast to 0.1", reconnaissance spectrum).
7. Before any novelty wording, search the exact Gaia DR3 ID in VizieR/CDS, ADS full text, arXiv astro-ph.EP, NEA TCE tables, KOI/TOI/CTOI; email authors of any hit.

**Phase C — publication (choose by what you have)**
8. **Validation-grade evidence or a professional co-author**: refereed paper. **AJ** (Tier 1 ≤30 quanta ≈ 7,000 words + ~10 figures/tables for $1,425; references/acknowledgements free; request the Publication Support Fund *at submission*; 15% if an AAS member) or **MNRAS** (£2,356; fully OA; India-based corresponding author → OUP 25% automatic LMIC discount per ONOS, confirm MNRAS eligibility; discretionary waiver form at submission, never in the cover letter; a co-author's Read-and-Publish institution as corresponding author covers the APC) or **PASP** ($142/typeset page non-OA). Write a full methods section so this paper is the "previously published methodology" for later RNAAS/CTOI filings. Placeholders KIC/TIC; after acceptance email exonames@ipac.caltech.edu for a Kepler number only if confirmed/validated.
9. **Candidate only**: (a) publish the *pipeline* (injection–recovery, inverted-null calibration, blind re-detection of known KOIs/TOIs, centroid/PRF localisation) in Astronomy & Computing (subscription route, no fee), PASP or AJ; (b) then an **RNAAS** (≤1,500 words incl. references, one figure, first-person singular, AASTeX 7, no line numbers, AI disclosure at submission + end-of-article statement naming tools and tasks, Zenodo DOI for data) that *cites* the methods paper — the structure ExoFOP's rule rewards and Mason et al. used; (c) within days request ExoFOP upload access, file `TIC NNNN.01` with all four parameters, paper URL, tag, note, light-curve figure.
10. **arXiv**: post the refereed paper (astro-ph.EP) once endorsed; RNAAS-length notes risk the three-page hold — post an expanded version or skip.
11. **Planet Hunters TESS Talk** (TESS-field candidates): post TIC + ephemeris, ask whether the team will include it in their CTOI/paper workflow; cite Eisner et al.; contact@zooniverse.org for credit.

**Phase D — what not to do (learned this month)**
- Do not submit an RNAAS on an unvalidated single candidate with an unpublished pipeline, two days after a viral "planet" post, with no professional having looked at it (AAS82222 pattern; declined in ≤11 h at moderation).
- Do not call ExoFOP registration "submitting a CTOI" in public; since 2026-08-19 it requires a paper URL and prior approval.
- Do not cite the TESS TOI FAQ as the CTOI procedure; it is stale.
- Do not treat catalogue absence as novelty; check membership/variability tables (Barber-style), KOI/TCE/TOI/CTOI, and ask the authors.
- Do not present the TGLC positive depth without the negative aperture depths (Kuber's own audit).
- Do not package the only public record as a Software-type repo zip; put a PDF in a Preprint record.
