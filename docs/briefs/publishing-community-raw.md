# Publishing & community brief: how an unaffiliated person gets an archival planet candidate recognised in October 2026

Checked live on 2026-10-17 (UTC) unless stated. Sources are inline. Where a page could not be reached from the sandbox (Reddit, Bluesky, X reply threads, OUP country tables via WebFetch, A&A directly) I say so and give what could be verified by other routes.

---

## 0. Headline findings (read this first)

1. **The CTOI shortcut is closed.** ExoFOP paused all community-candidate uploads on **31 March 2026** and reopened on **19 August 2026** with a new rule: *"candidates must first be published in a peer-reviewed journal with online access before being uploaded to ExoFOP, and approval to upload candidates is required"* (ExoFOP news, https://exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/tess/news.php; Candidate Guidelines, https://exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/tess/candidate_help.php). An RNAAS note is accepted as the "publication" **only if** *"the methodology to detect and vet the candidate has been previously published in a peer-reviewed journal that is available online and is cited by the Research Note."* The downloaded CTOI table (5,138 rows, 2026-10-17) shows exactly 0 pre-2026-08-19 rows with a paper URL, then 1,173 rows on 2026-08-27 (Lafarga et al. 2026, MNRAS, RAVEN) and 1 row on 2026-10-08 (a Planet Hunters TESS volunteer citing an RNAAS published 2026-10-06). That last row is the template to copy.
2. **RNAAS is in scope for single candidates and does accept sole-author, independent, AI-assisted notes.** The 2017 scope statement explicitly lists *"discovery of a single exoplanet"* (https://journals.aas.org/news/scope-of-research-notes-of-the-american-astronomical-society/). In 2026 RNAAS published Kaplan (Jan 2026, sole author, TESS M-dwarf candidate, DOI 10.3847/2515-5172/ae3758) and Rennell (Jul 2026, *"Independent Researcher, Anchorage, Alaska"*, acknowledgments: *"OpenAI Codex assisted with code generation, reproducibility checks, and manuscript drafting"*, DOI 10.3847/2515-5172/ae91dc). So Kuber's decline was **not** because the venue rejects candidates, independents, or AI disclosure per se.
3. **Why Kuber's note (AAS82222) was declined is not recorded anywhere in his repo**; his own post-decision audit implicitly identifies the editor's concerns as (a) how the sample was built, (b) what happened to the other 30 first-screen signals, (c) the TESS non-detection, (d) the Barber et al. 2022 catalogue match (prior-search status). The most plausible proximate causes (inference, labelled below) are: a 1,200-word note that spent most of its budget on caveats about an **unvalidated, FPP=0.069** signal whose own null test (inverted light curves) passes noise as often as data; a methodology that was itself unpublished (the exact thing ExoFOP's rule now forbids for CTOIs); and a sole unaffiliated author with disclosed heavy AI use submitting two days after a viral tweet that said "Found a new planet".
4. **NASA Exoplanet Archive (NEA) only ingests accepted, peer-reviewed papers** (https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/exoplanet_criteria.html; contribute page: *"All data or values must come from a peer-reviewed and accepted publication"*). There is no "candidate registration" channel for Kepler in 2026: the KOI tables are frozen at DR25; new Kepler-field planets since 2022 entered NEA exclusively through refereed papers (ExoMiner AJ 2023: 69 planets; Wang et al. 2024 MNRAS: 5; Ofir et al. 2025 AJ; Nabbie et al. 2025 Nat. Astron.; Lafarga et al. 2026 MNRAS) and were then given Kepler numbers by NExScI (exonames@ipac.caltech.edu). TAP check: KIC 5689121 / TIC 120686465 appears in none of `ps`, `toi`, `cumulative`.
5. **The realistic route for a Kepler-Bonus background-star candidate**: (i) Zenodo preprint with DOI the day you have a vetted ephemeris; (ii) a *methods + candidate* refereed paper (AJ/MNRAS/PASP) or a short refereed paper citing a published pipeline, co-authored with at least one professional; (iii) only then ExoFOP upload as a "Community Candidate" with Discovery Data Source = Kepler (ExoFOP now supports *"TESS, Kepler, K2, and beyond"*); (iv) NEA ingestion is automatic for accepted papers; a Kepler number comes only when the object is confirmed/validated (FPP < 1%).

### 1.2 What the audit addressed (= the editor's concerns, by inference)
The audit says it *"addresses the selection, other search outcomes, TESS non-detection and Barber catalogue reference"* and *"does not validate the candidate or establish a publication-ready revised analysis."* Its four sections:

### 1.3 Likely editorial reasons (my inference, ranked; none is documented)
- **Substance/scope**: RNAAS is *"moderated by an editor for appropriateness and format"* and is *"not peer reviewed"* (https://journals.aas.org/research-note-preparation-guidelines/). Lintott (lead editor) can decline on appropriateness alone within hours; Kuber's note arrived with FPP = 0.069 (not validated), a null-control that passes noise as often as data, a TESS non-recovery, an unresolved prior-search question, and a methodology (pipeline, thresholds, holdout design) that has never been refereed. A moderator reading "two inverted stars still pass at the candidate's strength" would reasonably judge the note premature.
- **The note undercut itself**: ~40% of the text is caveats ("not an independent holdout test", "not calibrated probabilities", "Novelty remains provisional"). That is honest, but a Research Note is supposed to communicate a result; an editor may conclude there is no result yet.
- **Novelty/overclaim mismatch**: the tweet said "planet ... went undiscovered" while the manuscript said "I do not ... claim that the source was never previously searched". Editors do look at public claims; this is speculation but a known reputational pattern (see §7).
- **AI/authorship**: AAS LLM guidelines (page states *"These guidelines were initially posted on 9 September 2026"*, https://journals.aas.org/author-llm-guidelines/) require disclosure and human responsibility; Kuber complied. Rennell's July 2026 note shows Codex-assisted independent notes are accepted. So AI disclosure alone is unlikely to be the reason, but *"OpenAI ChatGPT/Codex and Anthropic Claude assisted ... scientific synthesis"* plus a sole author with no track record raises the bar the moderator applies to whether a human actually stands behind the analysis.
- **Format**: not a factor — 1,203 words < 1,500, one multi-panel figure, abstract 124 < 150 words, first-person singular as required.

### 1.5 Live RNAAS and AAS policy (checked 2026-10-17)
- Scope (2017-10-16 statement): *"works in progress, comments and clarifications, null results"*, *"timely reports of observations"*, and results that *"would not traditionally merit a full paper (such as the discovery of a single exoplanet ...)"* (https://journals.aas.org/news/scope-of-research-notes-of-the-american-astronomical-society/).
- Limits: *"1,500 words or fewer"* including *"title, headers, captions, and references"*; 150 of those reserved for the mandatory abstract; *"no more than a single figure or table (but not both)"*; *"typically published within 72 hours of manuscript receipt"*; *"free to read and currently carry no author publication charges"*; CC-BY 4.0; max 20 Notes per first author per 12 months; *"single author papers is first person singular"*; no line numbers; AASTeX v7 preferred (https://journals.aas.org/research-note-preparation-guidelines/). The main RNAAS page still describes it as *"a non-peer reviewed, indexed and secure record of works in progress"* moderated by Chris Lintott (chris.lintott@aas.org) (https://journals.aas.org/research-notes/).
- Volume: Crossref lists 288 RNAAS items published 2026-01-01 → 2026-10-17 (api.crossref.org query, 2026-10-17).
- AI policy (https://journals.aas.org/author-llm-guidelines/): *"At submission, authors are required to disclose whether or not AI/LLMs were used in manuscript preparation"*; *"LLMs may not be used to generate any portion of a manuscript unsupervised"*; *"AI cannot be listed as an author"*; *"Publishing fictitious references is regarded as a serious professional breach"*; disclosure goes *"at the end of the article"* and figure captions must name the tool (and ideally the prompt); the guidelines *"concern the manuscript, not the data analysis"*; sanctions *"up to and including retraction ... and banning"*. The page gives templates, including a GAIDeT-taxonomy style statement (*"the following tasks were delegated to GAI tools under full human supervision: Code optimization, Visualization, Proofreading and editing"*).
- Ethics (https://journals.aas.org/professional-and-ethical-standards-for-the-aas-journals/): authorship for those who *"contributed significantly to the inception, design, execution, or interpretation of the research"*; *"The list of authors establishes accountability as well as credit."* No affiliation requirement anywhere; Kuber's "Independent researcher" was accepted by the portal (https://aas.msubmit.net/).
- arXiv and RNAAS: AAS's 2019 letter to arXiv records that arXiv moderators enforce a *"three pages"* minimum and often decline RNAAS-length notes (https://journals.aas.org/news/rnaas-on-arxiv/). Practical consequence: an RNAAS note may not be arXiv-postable as-is.

## 3. The ExoFOP Community-candidate (CTOI) route in 2026

### 3.1 Registration (https://exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/account.php, read 2026-10-17)
*"You need to be conducting astronomical observational, modelling, or theoretical work at a level consistent with publication in a professional journal and relevant to follow-up studies of exoplanet candidates. Please include the name of the university, research center or similar facility with which you are affiliated."* Form fields: First/Last name, **Affiliation (required)**, Position dropdown {Faculty/staff, Postdoc, Grad student, Undergrad, **Citizen Scientist**, Other}, Email, Phone. *"registration is not instantaneous"*; manual approval by email; agree to the ExoFOP Professional Conduct Policy. All uploads are public. TFOP WG membership is separate (https://tess.mit.edu/followup/apply-join-tfop/).

### 3.2 Submitting a candidate (https://exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/tess/candidate_help.php)
- Step 0: *"complete the Published Candidate Upload Request. After approval, then you can submit candidates."* (https://exofop.ipac.caltech.edu/tess/pub_candidate_upload_request.php — login required.)
- *"ExoFOP will only allow upload of Community Candidates accepted and published in the refereed literature. The URL link to the paper must be provided."*
- Two candidate types: **cTOI** (TESS discovery source) and **Other Discovery Data Source** (*"discovered by projects other than TESS"*; e.g. HARPS, NGTS — and explicitly Kepler/K2 in the news item).
- Required fields: TIC-based name `TID.nn` (next in sequence); Discovery source (TESS default; uncheck and enter e.g. "Kepler"); optional Candidate Name (*"should not be assigned a lowercase letter ('b', 'c', etc) until they are confirmed in the published, refereed literature"*); initial disposition PC or FP (CP not allowed); **at least two of** period (d), transit midpoint (BJD), depth (ppm) > 0; **for TOI-WG consideration all four of period, epoch, depth, duration**; a Tag; **Paper URL (required)**; a descriptive Note (required); then upload supporting files (light-curve figure) under the same tag. Cross-check against TOIs, CTOIs and confirmed planets; duplicates lead to removal/suspension. Period multiples of an existing candidate → add parameters to the existing object instead. Initial parameters are frozen; updates go in as additional parameter sets.
- Contact: exofop-support@ipac.caltech.edu.

### 3.3 What happens after
- The TOI team *"periodically checks for matches on TIC ID and period between the CTOI list hosted on ExoFOP and the comprehensive collection of TCEs"*; *"The vetting team may promote a CTOI to a TOI if the CTOI identifies a quality planet candidate from the SPOC or QLP pipeline mistakenly ruled out in the triage or vetting process"*; CTOIs with no SPOC/QLP TCE *"would need to be recovered manually in a reprocessing step"* (Guerrero et al. 2021, §8.4, arXiv:2103.12538, PDF extracted). Prime-mission stat: 169 of 841 CTOIs became TOIs. TOI FAQ: *"The TESS TOI Team then reviews each one, and candidates that meet its standard receive a TOI number"* (https://tess.mit.edu/toi-releases/toi-release-faqs/).
- Live numbers (downloaded CTOI CSV 2026-10-17): 5,138 CTOIs, **814 promoted to TOI (15.8%)**; TFOPWG dispositions almost all blank. Promotion rates by submitter: Planet Hunters TESS (user `eisner`) 119/182 = 65%; Olmschenk 165/184; Montalto 155/1,076; Chopra/Rabtsevich/Kuber TICs (370612437, 252721481, 4206066, 120686465) are **not** in the CTOI or TOI lists.
- TOI → TFOP follow-up → "CP" if confirmed/validated; TESS planet names get the TOI number (Guerrero §8.5).
- **Credit rule (TFOP WG Publication Policy v21, 2026-07-12, https://tess.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/TFOPWG_Publication_Policy_v21_20260712.pdf)**: *"In the special case of a community TOI (cTOI) that has been identified first by a member of the community and has been submitted to ExoFOP more than two months prior to its posting by the TESS project, the person or representatives of the team who provided ... the cTOI should be invited to join as an author on any publication pertaining to that cTOI for the first time. The cTOI must have included a transit epoch and transit duration (and optionally an orbital period) that coincides with the transit in the publication."* This is the only written guarantee of credit — and it now requires a prior publication to even file the CTOI.

### 3.4 Worked examples
- **Mason et al. 2026 (PHT volunteer-led)**: RNAAS "A Neptune Sized Planet Candidate Orbiting K-dwarf TIC 392664683" received/accepted Sept 2026, published 2026-10-06 (authors: *"Planet Hunters TESS Citizen Scientist, University of Oxford"* ×6 + Aigrain, Lintott, Platymesi), tools LATTE, lightkurve, TESS Centroid Vetting, pyaneti, TRICERATOPS, ARIADNE; CTOI 392664683.01 uploaded 2026-10-08 by user `mason` with the RNAAS URL, note *"PHT Community Candidate"*. Two days from publication to registry. The methods clause was satisfied because the PHT pipeline is published (Eisner et al. 2021, MNRAS).
- **Kaplan 2026**: sole-author RNAAS (Jan 2026; affiliation Sivas Science and Technology University), TESS M-dwarf candidate TIC 22760643.01 from a SPOC TCE, BLS + odd/even + secondary + centroid + batman; *"submitted to the ExoFOP as TIC 22760643.01 under Community Planet Candidates"* on 2026-01-06 (pre-pause; no paper URL field then); not promoted to TOI as of today.
- **Lafarga et al. 2026 (MNRAS, RAVEN)**: 1,173 CTOIs bulk-uploaded 2026-08-27 with paper URL — the first post-reopening upload.
- Chopra's `docs/GUIDE.md` (checked 2026-10-09 per the file) records the change: *"since 19 August 2026, ExoFOP only accepts community candidates that are already published"*; *"An earlier version of this guide said to submit CTOIs directly; that is no longer possible."* His `docs/CTOI_DRAFT.md` is a ready-made field list (period ± err, epoch BJD_TDB ± err, depth ppm, T14, Rp/R*, b, SNR, SDE, sectors, caveats) marked *"Cannot be uploaded yet."*

### 3.5 Can a Kepler-field candidate be a CTOI?
Yes as a **Community Candidate**, once published: the 2026-08-19 news says ExoFOP *"reopened support for community planet candidates from TESS, Kepler, K2, and beyond"*, and the form's Discovery Data Source can be set to Kepler. It will only be *considered by the TOI working group* if filed as a TESS-source cTOI with all four parameters — i.e. only if the signal is actually detected in TESS data. For KIC 5689121 the TESS fold is a non-recovery (−483 to −2,075 ppm apertures; +450±1,793 ppm TGLC), so it cannot honestly be filed as a TESS cTOI; it would be a Kepler-source Community Candidate (TIC 120686465.01) after a refereed paper. Note every CTOI row today has Discovery Data Source = TESS; the non-TESS path has no precedent yet.

## 4. Kepler/K2 candidates in 2026: is there a channel?

- **No live KOI process.** NEA's KOI documentation last updated 2018 lists "Q1-17 DR 25" as the latest delivery; the cumulative table is *"a superset of KOIs from all available quarters"* (https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/Kepler_KOI_docs.html). Nothing can be added to it.
- **The channel that works: refereed paper → NEA → Kepler number.** NEA TAP (2026-10-17): Kepler-facility planets with `disc_year ≥ 2024` come from Wang et al. 2024 (MNRAS 534, 1913; KOI-4978 b, Kepler-1489 c, Kepler-158 d, Kepler-879 c, Kepler-963 c), Ofir et al. 2025 (AJ 169, 90; Kepler-279 e, Kepler-289 e), Nabbie et al. 2025 (Nat. Astron.; KOI-134 b, c), Sun et al. 2025 (Nat. Astron.; Kepler-725 c), Lafarga et al. 2026 (MNRAS; TOI-4588 b). Earlier: Valizadegan et al. 2022/2023 (ExoMiner) — **370** planets in `ps` carry those refs and all got Kepler-NNNN names. So an ML/agent-validated set of Kepler candidates in a refereed journal **does** get ingested and named.
- **Kepler naming policy** (https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/KeplerNumbers.html, last updated 2022-01-24): use KOI/KIC placeholders in the manuscript; *after acceptance* email exonames@ipac.caltech.edu with the acceptance letter, accepted manuscript, object list (KOI and KIC/KepID), number of planets per star; numbers *"usually assigned within two business days"*; eligibility is *"confirmed or validated"*, decided by NExScI science staff for borderline cases. The page requires KIC designations, which a KBONUS background source (KIC 5689121 exists as a KIC entry; many Gaia-only background sources will not) may or may not satisfy — unresolved for Gaia-only hosts.
- **K2 policy** (https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/K2Numbers.html): candidates are named `EPIC NNNNNNNNN.NN` by the discoverer (check the k2pandc table for period duplicates; identifiers never reused); K2 numbers only for confirmed/validated planets in accepted peer-reviewed papers. The k2pandc table *was* fed from community catalogue papers (including an RNAAS: Zink et al. 2019, "Catalog of New K2 Exoplanet Candidates from Citizen Scientists", 10.3847/2515-5172/ab0a02), so RNAAS candidate catalogs have been ingested into the K2 **candidate** table historically; whether NEA still does this for new K2 RNAAS catalogs is unverified.
- **NEA definitions** (FAQ https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/faq.html): *"There is not a separate disposition for validated planets"*; *"All planets added ... are categorized as confirmed according to the archive's exoplanet criteria"*; `soltype` distinguishes "Published Confirmed", "Kepler Project Candidate", "TESS Project Candidate"; refuted planets become "False Positive Planet"; letters *"assigned alphabetically, starting from b, typically in the order the planets are published"*; literature values come from *"accepted, peer-reviewed papers"*.
- **Kepler Bonus background hosts**: TAP shows no Kepler-facility planet on a Gaia-DR3-named host; the only non-Kepler/KOI-named Kepler hosts are KIC-named (Lillo-Box 2021, Murphy 2016, Wang 2015). A KBONUS-background validated planet would be a first; the KBONUS paper itself reports no candidates (arXiv:2310.17733; authors Martínez-Palomera (BAERI/NASA Ames), Hedges (NASA GSFC/UMBC), Dotson (NASA Ames) per the paper; current affiliations unverified).
- **Late Kepler precedents via human re-vetting**: Kepler-1649 c (Vanderburg et al. 2020, ApJL 893, L27) was a Robovetter false positive recovered by the Kepler False Positive Working Group; Kepler-90 i / Kepler-80 g (Shallue & Vanderburg 2018, AJ 155, 94) via deep learning on Kepler TCEs. Both: refereed paper first, Kepler number after.

## 7. Naming and claiming: what you may call it, and the cost of getting it wrong

- **Candidate**: anything with a transit-like ephemeris and basic vetting. ExoFOP: *"Planet candidates should not be assigned a lowercase letter ('b', 'c', etc) until they are confirmed in the published, refereed literature."* Use `TIC NNNN.01`, `KIC NNNN.01`/`EPIC NNNN.01`, or "a planet candidate associated with Gaia DR3 …".
- **Validated**: statistically validated with FPP < 0.015 and NFPP < 0.001 (TRICERATOPS, Giacalone et al. 2021, AJ 161, 24; eumemic correctly notes the radius restriction), or vespa/other FPP < 1%, **plus** the input constraints (contrast curve, spectroscopy) actually obtained — and **published in a refereed journal**. NEA then lists it as "confirmed" (*"There is not a separate disposition for validated planets"*) and assigns the letter; Kepler/K2/TESS numbers are assigned by NExScI/the TESS project after acceptance, never by the discoverer.
- **Confirmed**: dynamical mass (RV/TTV) or equivalent; TESS catalog "CP" includes *"statistically validated planets that do not yet have measured masses"* (Guerrero 2021 §9.1).
- **Named**: IAU designations are *"the catalogue name of the host star followed by a letter"*; *"no unified naming convention for exoplanets exists"*; public names only via NameExoWorlds (https://iauarchive.eso.org/public/themes/naming_exoplanets/). You do not get to name it.
- **"New"/"undiscovered"**: only claim absence from *specific* catalogues on a *specific* date (Kuber's manuscript did this correctly; his tweet did not). Rabtsevich's "nobody knew existed" was contradicted in print within a week by Mercader Perez (already flagged in 2020, selected by LEO-Vetter in early 2026).
- **Reputational mechanics observed this month**: the tweet-vs-repo gap ("Found a new planet" vs *"No confirmed planet is claimed"*) is exactly what moderators and professionals check; AI-styled prose draws hostile attention irrespective of content (HN); an RNAAS decline after a public "planet" claim is now part of the public record (README line 1). Conversely, Rabtsevich's explicit *"No statistical validation is claimed"* + DDT proposal + professionals quoted in Sci Am produced goodwill even though the candidate was not new to the TESS team.
- **Recommended wording ladder**: "transit-like signal" → "planet candidate (unvalidated)" → "statistically validated planet candidate (FPP = x, inputs y)" → "validated planet" (after refereed acceptance) → "confirmed planet" (after mass). Never skip a rung in a title, tweet or README.

## 8. Concrete submission plan, per venue, for a Claude-Code-found archival candidate

**Phase A — day 0 (the moment the ephemeris survives your own null tests)**
1. Zenodo record, type *Preprint* or *Dataset*, CC-BY, with: ephemeris (P, T0 in BJD_TDB with uncertainties), predicted future transit windows (UTC, with barycentric correction), depth/duration, host IDs, the vetting table, and the environment lock. Reserve the DOI; version later. (Free; Rabtsevich's record drew 7k views.)
2. Pre-register the tests you will run on any *future* data (TESS sector, DDT) before they exist; Chopra's `*_predicted_transits.csv` is a template.
3. Do **not** tweet "planet". If you post, post the Zenodo DOI with the word "candidate" and the FPP.

**Phase B — weeks 1–4: make the candidate admissible**
4. Email one collaborator group (§6) with the evidence pack; parallel: file an arXiv endorsement request only once someone has read the work.
5. If the host is TESS-observable and the signal is TESS-detectable, submit a **TESS DDT** (2-min cadence; ≥6 weeks before sector; tess-ddt@mit.edu; no proprietary period) — it costs nothing, creates a public record (approved list), and worked for Rabtsevich. For a G=16 Kepler-field star it will not help (Kuber's injection test: ~987 ppm increment vs 1,294 ppm scatter).
6. Run TRICERATOPS with *measured* inputs; if FPP < 0.015 & NFPP < 0.001 and Rp < 8 R⊕, you have a validation-grade result; otherwise write it as a candidate and say what observation would close it (AO contrast to 0.1", reconnaissance spectrum).

**Phase C — publication (choose by what you have)**
7. **If you have validation-grade evidence or a professional co-author**: write a refereed paper. Venue choice: **AJ** (discovery/validation standard; Tier 1 ≤30 quanta = ~7,000 words + ~10 figures/tables for $1,425; ask about the AAS Publication Support Fund at submission) or **MNRAS** (£2,356 APC; or free if the RAS/OUP discretionary waiver is granted — apply *at submission*, never in the cover letter; no affiliation requirement) or **PASP** (methods-heavy; $142/typeset page non-OA). Include a full methods section so the paper itself becomes the "previously published methodology" for later RNAAS/CTOI filings. Use KIC/TIC placeholders; after acceptance email exonames@ipac.caltech.edu for a Kepler number only if the planet is confirmed/validated.
8. **If you have a candidate only**: (a) first publish the *pipeline* (methods, injection–recovery, inverted-null calibration, blind re-detection of known KOIs/TOIs) in a refereed venue — Astronomy & Computing (subscription route, no fee) or PASP or AJ; (b) then an **RNAAS** (≤1,500 words all-in, one figure, first-person singular, AI disclosure at the end naming tools and tasks, AASTeX 7, no line numbers, Zenodo DOI for data) that *cites* the methods paper — this exact structure is what ExoFOP's rule rewards and what Mason et al. did; (c) within days, request ExoFOP upload access, file `TIC NNNN.01` with all four parameters, paper URL, tag, note, light-curve figure.
9. **arXiv**: post the refereed paper (astro-ph.EP) once endorsed; RNAAS-length notes risk the three-page hold — post an expanded version or skip.
10. **Planet Hunters TESS Talk**: for TESS-field candidates, post the TIC and ephemeris on Talk and ask the PHT team whether they will include it in their CTOI/paper workflow — this is the only channel that routinely results in co-authorship for outsiders with a published pipeline attached.

**Phase D — what not to do (learned this month)**
- Do not submit an RNAAS on an unvalidated single candidate with an unpublished pipeline, two days after a viral "planet" post, with no professional having looked at it (AAS82222 pattern).
- Do not call ExoFOP registration "submitting a CTOI" in public; since 2026-08-19 it requires a paper URL.
- Do not treat catalogue absence as novelty; check Barber-style membership tables, KOI/TCE/TOI/CTOI, and ask the relevant authors.
- Do not present the TGLC positive depth without the negative aperture depths (Kuber's own audit).
