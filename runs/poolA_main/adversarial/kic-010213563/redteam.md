## Verdict: weak (as a signal on KIC 10213563 it is effectively dead; as a transit-like signal in the blend it survives)

The light-curve signal itself survives everything I threw at it: blind recovery, 36/36 positive events, no odd/even difference (1.0 sigma), no secondary (-96 +- 70 ppm at phase 0.5, nothing coherent elsewhere), no gap/ramp/quarter-edge dependence, no stellar-variability alias, no inverted-flux counterpart, no ephemeris match with any KOI/TCE/EB, the TPF host KIC 10213566 excluded at >7 sigma, and a flat-bottomed shape. **But the pixel-level localisation does not land on the target.** With the WCS calibrated on the bright host, the missing light in every season (four channels) is centred 1.2-2 arcsec from the target's Gaia position, on the side of an unmodelled G 18.35 Gaia source 0.97 arcsec away (Gaia DR3 2128591018880005120, absent from the KBONUS catalogue, so its light is inside the target's "PSF flux"), with the target position disfavoured by dchi2 = 27-108 per season (four seasons, four channels, WCS calibrated to 0.1 px) and a model-light column-ratio test agreeing in every season. The neighbour is the best catalogued hypothesis; a source at or just beyond the stamp edge is not excluded. On top of that, the PSF depth differs between Kepler seasons at chi2 = 14.7/3 (a hallmark of season-dependent flux assignment for a blended edge-of-stamp source) and the transit duration excludes the catalogue 0.79 Rsun K dwarf on a circular orbit (rho* = 0.98 +0.53/-0.62 g/cc, 99% < 1.8 g/cc, vs 2.3 g/cc; dchi2 = 25). The host is therefore ambiguous between the target (then a ~5 Re object, with the depth undiluted by the neighbour and carrying a +-20% season systematic) and the fainter neighbour (then a ~0.9% event: a giant planet, brown dwarf or diluted EB on a star of unknown type), with the pixels favouring the latter. Nothing here should be promoted to "candidate" for KIC 10213563; the honest statement is "a transit-like signal in the 1-arcsec blend KIC 10213563 + Gaia DR3 2128591018880005120, more likely on the fainter component".

## 6. Pixel-level localisation (own code)

Method (`pixel_free_fit.py`, then `pixel_wcs_calibrated.py`): per event, per-pixel linear out-of-transit baseline (0.75-1.5 T14 either side, within 1.5 d) minus the in-transit core (|dt| < 0.4 T14), inverse-variance stacked per quarter and per season; then a single-Gaussian "missing light" model with its position free over -2.5 to nx+2.5 px (i.e. allowed beyond the stamp), amplitude linear, PSF sigma from the images. The WCS and the PSF width were calibrated per season by fitting the out-of-transit image with Gaussians at the Gaia positions of the host, target, neighbour and four other stars with a common (dx, dy) shift: dx, dy = (-0.02, -0.10), (-0.09, -0.01), (-0.12, +0.06) px for seasons 0, 1, 2; sigma 0.52-0.60 px. So the Gaia-to-pixel mapping is good to ~0.1 px; the (Gaussian) PSF model is crude (reduced chi2 ~850 on the 25,000 e/s host) and that is the main systematic.

## 9. Pipeline notes (not part of the verdict)

1. `pixel_vet.py`'s significance-weighted deficit centroid cannot move beyond the stamp edge and its `chi2_gauss_at_target` is only compared with a scaled-image model; it never tests a Gaia neighbour inside 1 px or a free position. For this star the per-quarter `chi2_gauss_at_source` for the 0.97" neighbour was already lower than for the target in 9 of 12 quarters in `pixel.json`, but no rule looks at that. Add a free-position PRF fit with a WCS calibration on the brightest star and report dchi2(target) - dchi2(best).
2. KBONUS source-catalogue completeness: Gaia sources within ~1.5" of a target that KBONUS did not model should be flagged at triage (`crowdsap`/`psffrac` do not know about them). The CROWDSAP keywords (0.83-0.97) describe the wrong aperture for this star (rederivation, section 3).
3. Per-event depth with a one-sided linear baseline misbehaves at quarter edges (event 0 here: 6880 vs 2475 ppm). Use a flat or symmetric baseline, or skip events whose window is truncated.
4. Season-dependent depth (chi2 14.7/3) is an informative blend diagnostic for edge-of-stamp PSF photometry and should be a reported statistic, not only per-year consistency.
5. A 3.5-MAD upward clip before the phase scan matters: with SAP_QUALITY == 0 alone the KBONUS flux still contains +9% (BKJD 200.19) and +1.4% (256.5) excursions that produced false 8-sigma "features" in my first unclipped scan.
